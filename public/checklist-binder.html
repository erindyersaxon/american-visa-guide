Interview Binder Checklist

How to organize your documents for the embassy. Print-friendly reference.

Why a binder? The embassy doesn't require one, but it helps you stay organized and shows the officer you're taking the process seriously. Keep it clean, avoid excessive highlighting, and include a simple table of contents.

0 of 45 items checked

Front section: immediate access Medical · Doc check · Interview

Section 1: Medical documents Medical + doc check

Section 2: Civil documents (originals + copies) Doc check + interview backup

Section 3: Financial documents (copies) Interview backup only

All financial documents must already be uploaded to CEAC. The officer will not accept new documents at the interview window.

Section 4: Identity & travel documents Interview backup

Section 5: Supporting / case-specific As needed

Section 6: Relationship evidence (rarely requested) Prepare anyway as a safeguard

Rarely requested at the interview, but increased community experiences suggest you should prepare this anyway as a safeguard.

Organization tips:

  • Use clear, adhesive tabs for each section (not loose sticky notes)
  • Include a table of contents at the front
  • Avoid excessive highlighting; use simple yellow only for key dates
  • Keep originals in the binder; bring copies separately
  • Organize documents chronologically within each section
  • Include brief labels or notes to help the officer navigate

Emotional Support Binders

Organization matters: having documents in labeled sections makes the document check faster and less stressful. Members call it the emotional support binder: when the officer asks for a document, you know which tab holds it. Here are six setups that members have used:

Whichever you choose, give public charge its own tab. Print your public charge statement and every file of your evidence pack, in the same order and with the same codes as your CEAC upload: 00 for the statement, P for the petitioner, J for a joint sponsor, B for you. Community moderators report officers asking beneficiaries for their own CV, diplomas, bank statements and assets, and members preparing for re-interviews bring all of it. When the officer names a document, you find it by its code.

Accordion folder (Erin)
Compact, lightweight, good for medical day. Color-coded tabs for each section. Available at Staples.
Ring binder with dividers (Erin)
More capacity for originals and copies. Printed tabs: Medical, Doc Check (civil documents go here), Petitioner, Joint Sponsor, Cub and Bean (the children's birth certificates, passports and CRBAs), US Domicile (petitioner and joint sponsor both), Prev Visas, USCIS Notices. Binder from Staples, dividers from Amazon.
Expanding file with clear pockets (Chris)
Clear plastic pockets inside an expanding file, one pocket per topic. Handwritten tabs, in order: Intro, Public Charge, ID/Marriage, Police Checks, Employment, Savings/Tax, Retirement, Health Insurance, Joint Sponsor, Airbnb, Degree, Medical Checks. A tab for each topic in the public charge statement lets you pull out the proof as you answer. Chris brought this folder to his IR1 interview in October 2026: read Chris's interview account.
Expanding file with tabbed documents (Wyverex)
The tabs sit on the documents themselves, so each set stays together when you lift it out. Handwritten tabs include Marriage Docs, Divorce Docs, Tax Transcripts and I-864.
Mesh zip wallet (uncolouredhat)
A slim, clear zip wallet. Light enough to hold on your lap in the waiting room, and you can see the papers inside without opening it.
Zip portfolio with handle (Fish)
A faux-leather zip portfolio with a carry handle. It closes all the way round, so nothing slips out on the journey to the embassy.
