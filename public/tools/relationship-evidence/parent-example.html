Example · I-130, parent (IR5)

Parent evidence example

A finished record set for a fictional daughter and her parents. Emma Novak, 38, a US citizen in Seattle, files one I-130 for her mother, Margaret Collins, and a second for her father, David Collins, who live in Cardiff. Three civil records prove both relationships. The chapters match the Parent and child setting in the evidence template.

What makes it work
  • Emma meets the age rule. Only a US citizen aged 21 or older can file for a parent (8 CFR 204.2(f)(1)). Her naturalization certificate proves citizenship, and her birth certificate shows her age.
  • Each parent has a petition of their own. A parent's spouse cannot come as a derivative, so Margaret and David each need an I-130 (8 CFR 204.2(f)(4)).
  • The father's petition carries one more record. For a mother, the birth certificate naming her is enough. For a father, the parents' marriage certificate goes with it, plus proof that any earlier marriage of either parent ended (8 CFR 204.2(f)(2)(i)).
  • Emma's own name change is explained. She files as Emma Novak, so her marriage certificate goes in both petitions (8 CFR 204.2(f)(2)(v)).
  • No photos, no messages and no affidavits. A child born to married parents proves the relationship with civil records.
Is this your case?
  • You are under 21: you cannot file for a parent yet.
  • Your parents were not married when you were born, and you are filing for your father: you also need proof of legitimation before 18, or of a real relationship with him before you turned 21 (8 CFR 204.2(f)(2)(ii)–(iii)). See the parent-child example.
  • You are filing for a stepparent: they must have married your parent before you turned 18. See the stepchild example for the records.
  • You are filing for an adoptive parent: the adoption must have taken place before you turned 16, with 2 years of custody and living together (8 CFR 204.2(f)(2)(iv)). See the adopted child example.
  • Your parents have other children abroad: they cannot come as derivatives either. A US citizen can file for a brother or sister separately; see the sibling example.

Plan the next stage now

The I-130 only proves the relationship. At the NVC stage Emma will sponsor each parent on an affidavit of support, and the embassy will weigh public charge factors such as age, health and health insurance. See public charge for parents of US citizens.
