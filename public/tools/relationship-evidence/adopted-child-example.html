Example · I-130, adopted child (IR2)

Adopted child evidence example

A finished record set for a fictional mother and daughter. Rachel Kim, a US citizen who has lived in Manchester since 2015, adopted Lily with her husband Tom in 2022. The family is moving to the US, and Rachel files an I-130 for Lily, now 11. Seven records prove the adoption, the custody and two years of living together. The chapters match the Parent and child setting in the evidence template. Drawn faces stand in for real photos.

Check the Hague rules first

The UK is a party to the Hague Adoption Convention. A US citizen living in the US cannot use an I-130 for a child adopted from a Convention country after the Convention took effect; that adoption goes through the Hague process on Forms I-800A and I-800 instead (8 CFR 204.2(d)(2)(vii)(D)–(F)).

Rachel can file an I-130 because she lived in the UK and met the 2-year custody and residence rules by living with Lily there. The regulation then treats her as living outside the US when she adopted (8 CFR 204.2(d)(2)(vii)(E)). If your facts differ, read the Policy Manual on Hague restrictions before you file.

What makes it work
  • The adoption came before age 16. Lily was 6 when the court made the adoption order (8 CFR 204.2(d)(2)(vii)).
  • Legal custody is counted the conservative way. Only a court or government order counts, and with no custody order before the adoption, custody starts on the adoption order. A notarized custody affidavit would not count at all (8 CFR 204.2(d)(2)(vii)(A)). Rachel filed well after the 2 years were up.
  • The residence records show where everyone lived: Lily, Rachel and Tom at one address, and the birth parents elsewhere. The regulation asks for all three (8 CFR 204.2(d)(2)(vii)(B)).
  • The records show parental control, the examples the regulation gives: Rachel maintains the home, supports Lily and supervises her day to day.
  • Every record came from a court, a register office, the council, a school, a GP or HMRC. Two photo rows support them; they do not replace them.
Is this your case?
  • The adoption came after the child turned 16: it does not qualify, unless the child is the older sibling of a child you adopted under 16 and the adoption came before 18.
  • You have not yet had 2 years of custody and 2 years of living together: wait until you have both. Time before and after the adoption both count, and a break does not erase time already served (8 CFR 204.2(d)(2)(vii)(C)).
  • The child still lived with a birth parent during those 2 years: you must also show that you, not the birth parent, had primary parental control.
  • Your spouse is not a US citizen: Tom immigrates on his own I-130 as Rachel's spouse. A US citizen files a separate I-130 for each family member. See the I-130 checklist.
  • The petitioner lives abroad: plan the affidavit of support early. See domicile.
