Example · I-130, married son or daughter (F3)

Married son or daughter example

A finished record set for a fictional father and his married son. Ian Fraser, a US citizen in Boston, files an I-130 for his son, Callum Fraser, 34, who lives in Glasgow with his wife, Nadia, and their two children. Three civil records prove the relationship and the category, and Callum's family goes in Part 4. The chapters match the Parent and child setting in the evidence template.

What makes it work
  • Only a US citizen can file. F3 covers the married sons and daughters of US citizens, at any age. A permanent resident can petition only for unmarried sons and daughters (INA 203(a)(2)–(3)).
  • The father's petition carries the parents' marriage certificate. A mother needs only the birth certificate naming her. A father adds the marriage certificate and proof that any earlier marriage of either parent ended (8 CFR 204.2(d)(2)(i)).
  • Callum's own marriage certificate goes in too. It matches the marital status Ian gives on the I-130 and names the spouse he lists in Part 4.
  • Callum's family needs no petitions of their own. Nadia and the children are listed in Part 4 and share Callum's priority date (8 CFR 204.2(d)(4)).
  • No photos, no messages and no affidavits. A son born to married parents proves the relationship with civil records.
Is this your case?
  • Your son or daughter is unmarried: under 21 is IR2, and 21 or over is F1. The records are the same, without their marriage certificate. If they were married before, send the divorce or death record instead.
  • You are a permanent resident: you cannot file for a married son or daughter. If you file for an unmarried son or daughter (F2B) and they marry before immigrating, the petition can no longer be used.
  • Their marital status changes after you file: if an F3 son or daughter divorces, the petition becomes F1, or IR2 if they are under 21, and keeps its priority date (8 CFR 204.2(i)(1)(iii)). Tell USCIS or the National Visa Center.
  • You were not married to the other parent at the birth, and you are the father: see the parent-child example. Stepparents and adoptive parents: see the stepchild and adopted child examples.
  • F3 petitions wait years for a visa number. Check the Visa Bulletin for your country.
