Example · I-130, child (IR2)

Parent-child evidence example

A finished evidence pack for a fictional father and daughter. Marcus Reed, a US citizen in North Carolina, files an I-130 for Ella Bennett, 14, who lives in Manchester with her mother. Marcus and Ella's mother never married, so Marcus has to prove more than the birth certificate shows. Five records made at the time lead, then three photo rows cover 2012 to 2023. The chapters match the Parent and child setting in the evidence template. Drawn faces stand in for real photos.

What makes it work
  • Marcus proves two things. Ella's birth certificate names him, which shows he is her natural father. The other records show a real parent-child relationship while Ella was unmarried and under 21 (8 CFR 204.2(d)(2)(iii)).
  • Records made at the time lead. USCIS calls these the most persuasive evidence of a parent-child relationship. A register office, a bank, a school, Wise and an insurer issued them, not Marcus.
  • The records cover what the regulation asks about: Ella's support (14 years of monthly payments), her schooling (school records naming Marcus) and her welfare (life insurance and visits).
  • There is no gap. Every year from Ella's birth in 2012 to filing in 2026 has at least one record, including the ten years Marcus has lived in the US.
  • The two affidavits come from people who saw the relationship first-hand: Ella's mother and her head teacher. The regulation names school officials.
  • Photos come last, and there are only three. They support the records; they do not replace them.
  • No parents' marriage certificate. Marcus and Claire never married, so Ella was not legitimated by their marriage. The bona fide relationship carries the case.
Is this your case?
  • A mother filing, or a father who was married to the mother when the child was born, needs no proof of a bona fide relationship. See the I-130 checklist for the documents your category needs.
  • Marcus naturalized in 2021, after Ella was born. If the parent was already a US citizen when the child was born abroad, the child may already be a US citizen. Check If your child is a US citizen (CRBA) before you file an I-130.

Affidavits

Two sworn statements back up the records. Each gives the person's full name, address, date and place of birth, how they know Marcus and Ella, and the dates and places they saw them together.

  • Claire Bennett, Ella's mother. She describes Marcus at the birth, the monthly payments, his visits every summer and Christmas, and the weekly video calls.
  • Diane Holt, head teacher at Brookfield Primary School from 2016 to 2023. She describes Marcus at the admission meeting in 2016, at sports day in 2018, and at parents' evenings by video.
