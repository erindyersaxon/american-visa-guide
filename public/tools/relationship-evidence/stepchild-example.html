Example · I-130, stepchild (IR2)

Stepchild evidence example

A finished record set for a fictional stepfather and stepson. Michael Turner, a US citizen in Colorado, married Sarah in Bath in 2025. He files one I-130 for Sarah and a second for her son, Oliver Whitfield, 12. Four civil records prove the step relationship, and two photo rows show the family together. The chapters match the Parent and child setting in the evidence template. Drawn faces stand in for real photos.

What makes it work
  • The marriage date is the test. Michael married Sarah before Oliver turned 18, so Oliver is his stepchild (8 CFR 204.2(d)(2)(iv)). Oliver was 11.
  • Every link has a record: Oliver's birth certificate naming Sarah, the marriage certificate, and the divorces that ended both spouses' earlier marriages. The regulation asks for the earlier marriages of both the stepparent and the parent.
  • The regulation does not ask Michael to prove a bond with Oliver himself. The step relationship stands on the marriage, so the evidence that the marriage is real, filed with Sarah's I-130, supports Oliver's petition too.
  • Two photo rows show the three of them as a family, labeled with names and dates. They support the records; they do not replace them.
  • Oliver's petition goes in with a copy of the receipt notice for Sarah's, so USCIS can link the two.
Is this your case?
  • The marriage took place after the child turned 18: the child is not a stepchild for immigration. An I-130 on that basis cannot be approved.
  • The child is the petitioner's own child: see the I-130 checklist. A father not married to the mother at the birth also needs the parent-child evidence.
  • The petitioner is a permanent resident: Oliver can be listed in Part 4 of his mother's F2A petition instead of having his own (8 CFR 204.2(a)(4)). A US citizen files a separate I-130 for each child.
  • The marriage is less than 2 years old when Oliver is admitted: he gets a conditional green card, like his mother's.
