Stage 1: USCIS / I-130

If USCIS sends an RFE on your I-130

A Request for Evidence (RFE) means the officer could not approve your petition on what you sent. You get one chance to answer it. This page explains what a spouse RFE asks for and how to build a response that answers every item.

This is a peer resource, not legal advice. If your RFE questions an earlier marriage, a divorce or the facts of your relationship, have a US immigration attorney review your response before you send it. Find one through AILA Lawyer Search.

Four rules before you start

You get one response, with no extensions USCIS sets the deadline on the RFE. It cannot be longer than 12 weeks, and USCIS grants no extra time (8 CFR 103.2(b)(8)(iv)). If you miss it, USCIS can deny the petition as abandoned, on the record, or both (8 CFR 103.2(b)(13)(i)).
  1. Send everything at once. The regulation says "all requested materials must be submitted together at one time, along with the original USCIS request for evidence." If you send only part of it, USCIS treats that as a request to decide on the record you already have (8 CFR 103.2(b)(11)). Do not send a first batch and promise the rest later.
  2. Answer every item. If you cannot get a document, say so in your cover letter, explain why, and send the best substitute. Leaving an item out reads as a partial response.
  3. Your evidence must show you were eligible when you filed. USCIS denies a petition when the response "does not establish filing eligibility at the time the benefit request was filed" (8 CFR 103.2(b)(12)). If a prior divorce became final after your current wedding, USCIS "looks to the laws of the place where the parties celebrated the subsequent marriage" and issues a Notice of Intent to Deny if those laws do not make the marriage valid (6 USCIS-PM B.6(B)(4)). Talk to an attorney if that is your situation.
  4. Answer where the RFE tells you to. If you filed online, you answer in your USCIS online account. If you filed on paper, follow the instructions on the notice. See online or by mail.

Reading your RFE

Spouse RFEs that members have shared follow a standard layout. USCIS uses template wording, so a long RFE does not mean the officer suspects fraud. Look for these parts:

  • "What You Need to Do." This is your task list. Each bullet is an item you must answer. Number them, and use the same numbers in your cover letter.
  • A date range. For example, "Submit evidence of a shared life together from October 11, 2024 to the present." The start date is usually your marriage date. Your evidence needs to cover every month in that range.
  • Legal capacity to marry. Wording about prior marriages, divorce, "No Record" letters and Decree Nisi. See proof you were free to marry.
  • Categories of evidence. Shared Finances, Shared Residence, Children, Relationship History, Other Evidence. These are USCIS's own headings. Use them as the headings of your response.
  • Affidavit rules. What an affidavit must contain, and who may not write one.
  • Specific findings. Some RFEs also say what the officer found weak, for example: "You submitted payments... but there was no explanation or evidence of where the money was used. There is no evidence of comingled finances." Treat each finding as its own item and answer it head-on.

USCIS judges whether you meant "to establish a lasting relationship at the inception of the marriage" (6 USCIS-PM B.6). The petitioner carries the burden of proof, and the standard is a preponderance of the evidence: more likely than not (6 USCIS-PM B.6(C)(2)). Evidence from before the wedding still helps, because it shows how the relationship led to marriage.

Proof you were free to marry

The petition needs "proof of the legal termination of all previous marriages of both the petitioner and the beneficiary" (8 CFR 204.2(a)(2)).

If either of you was married before

  • Send a final divorce decree, a final annulment decree or a death certificate for every earlier marriage of either spouse (6 USCIS-PM B.6(C)(1)). "In all cases, the divorce must be final," and a decree nisi is "not acceptable evidence" (6 USCIS-PM B.6).
  • For a UK divorce, send the sealed final document. In England and Wales, that is the Final Order, called the Decree Absolute before April 2022, from HM Courts & Tribunals Service. In Scotland, it is the extract decree from National Records of Scotland. In Northern Ireland, it is the Decree Absolute from HM Courts & Tribunals Service Northern Ireland. The State Department lists "no alternate documents" for UK divorces, so you have no fallback: order the record (UK reciprocity schedule).
  • Check the dates. The final divorce date must come before the date of your current marriage.
  • If a divorce was granted abroad and you later married in the United States, "USCIS must verify that the foreign divorce is recognized in the jurisdiction of the later marriage." Some states recognize a foreign divorce only if one of the divorcing spouses was domiciled in the country that granted it (6 USCIS-PM B.6(B)(4)). If that applies to you, ask an attorney.

If neither of you was married before

The standard RFE asks for "letter(s) from the government agency authorized to issue such letters certifying that there is 'No Record' of marriage under their names." Answer for each spouse separately:

  • US citizen petitioner: ask the vital records office of each state where you lived as an adult whether it issues a no-record letter or a marriage search result.
  • UK beneficiary: send the document your RFE names. RFEs that members have shared accept a "Certificate of No Impediment" or a "marital status affirmation." GOV.UK's Get proof you're free to marry service issues both, online, to anyone with a valid British passport. The service is built for people planning a marriage abroad: it asks you to confirm you have checked the rules of the country where you will marry. If you are already married and the service does not fit, say so in your cover letter and send the best evidence you have of the beneficiary's marital history.
  • If a no-record letter is not available, say so in your cover letter, name the authority you asked, and attach its reply if it sent one. When a record does not exist, USCIS expects a written statement from the authority on its letterhead saying so, and why (1 USCIS-PM E.6).

If the marriage took place abroad

Send the full civil marriage certificate from the registry, with a certified English translation if needed (8 CFR 103.2(b)(3)). A ceremony or church certificate alone is not enough. If the marriage relies on a foreign law in an unusual way, for example a religious-only ceremony, the RFE may cite Matter of Annang and ask you to show the marriage complied with that law. Get an attorney's help for that item.

Children's birth certificates

An RFE may ask for "birth certificates for all biological children." That means every child either spouse listed on the I-130 or I-130A, including children from earlier relationships and children who will not immigrate.

  • Send the full (long-form) certificate that names both parents. A UK short certificate leaves the parents out.
  • For children you have together, the certificate is also bona fide evidence: it appears under "Children" in the standard list.
  • If a name or date differs from the forms, add the document that explains it, such as a deed poll, and a one-line note in the cover letter.

Evidence of a shared life

Organize this part by the RFE's own headings, and inside each heading by month. An officer should see every month in the date range covered by at least one document made at the time.

RFE headingWhat to send
Shared FinancesJoint bank account statements, plus a letter from the bank stating when the account was opened and when each name was added. Joint credit card statements. Joint tax returns. Joint loans or insurance. A mortgage or lease in both names.
Shared ResidenceMail to both of you at one address. Joint utility, council tax or phone bills. Jointly owned home, car or business. Insurance listing one spouse as the other's dependent. Driving licenses at the same address.
ChildrenBirth certificates listing both parents. Adoption certificates. School or medical records listing one spouse as an emergency contact for the other's children.
Relationship HistoryPhotos with friends and family, captioned with date and place. Travel itineraries and boarding passes for trips together. Wedding photos. A sample of cards, letters and messages.
Other EvidenceJoint memberships. Joint pet records. Receipts for gifts. Affidavits (see below).

If you live in different countries

Many couples on this route have not lived together yet. Say so in one sentence in the cover letter, so the officer reads the gaps in Shared Residence correctly. Then make the other headings carry the weight:

  • Every visit, with boarding passes, passport stamps and bookings. For US visits, print the beneficiary's CBP I-94 travel history.
  • A joint account that both of you use, with the bank's opening letter. A new account counts for more once it shows real activity.
  • Each spouse named on the other's life insurance, pension or health cover.
  • Plans to live together: job applications, flat or house searches, a shipping quote.
  • A sample of messages and call logs spread across the whole period. The relationship evidence template lays these out so an officer can read them quickly.

Money transfers

Transfers from one spouse to the other show financial support. Shared accounts, shared bills and shared assets show commingling. One RFE a member shared listed both gaps separately: payments with "no explanation or evidence of where the money was used," and "no evidence of comingled finances." More transfer records will not answer the second point.

How to explain transfers

Write a signed statement with a table. Give each transfer its own row:

  • Date, amount and currency
  • Who sent it and who received it
  • What it paid for: rent, flights for a visit, wedding costs, a deposit, living costs while one of you was out of work
  • The document that proves the use: a receipt, an invoice, a booking confirmation, or the recipient's bank statement showing the payment going out

A written explanation alone will not settle it. The Policy Manual says you must resolve doubts "by independent objective evidence," and that explanations "absent competent objective evidence... will not suffice" (1 USCIS-PM E.6). Add a short paragraph explaining why you used transfers rather than a joint account, for example because one of you could not yet open an account in the other's country. Then add the commingling evidence from Shared Finances.

Affidavits

Affidavits support documents. They do not replace them. The standard RFE says affidavits "will only be given minimal weight unless they are supported by other independent and objective evidence," and that affidavits from the petitioner or the beneficiary are not acceptable.

Each affidavit must be sworn or affirmed under penalty of perjury by someone with personal knowledge, and must include:

  • the writer's full name and address
  • the writer's date and place of birth
  • how the writer is related to, or knows, each of you
  • full details of what they saw, with dates and places
  • how they know it first-hand
  • a pointer to the documents that back it up, for example "I attended their wedding on [date]" next to the wedding photo that shows the writer

USCIS weighs each affidavit for detail, consistency and plausibility, and gives "little weight" to one whose writer lacks direct knowledge of what they describe. Officers compare affidavits with the rest of your evidence to look for inconsistencies (1 USCIS-PM E.6). Two or three detailed affidavits from people who have seen you together count for more than many short ones. The relationship evidence template has a section for them.

Putting the response together

  1. The RFE notice on top. 8 CFR 103.2(b)(11) requires it.
  2. A cover letter of one to two pages. List each numbered RFE item, then the exhibits that answer it. Add a short factual history of the relationship. Keep it factual: dates, places, documents.
  3. An exhibit index, grouped under the RFE's own headings.
  4. The exhibits, each labelled with its number and a one-line caption. Add a certified English translation to every document not in English (8 CFR 103.2(b)(3)).

Online or by mail

If you filed online, USCIS tells you about the RFE by text or email. Log in to your USCIS online account and go to the "Documents or Notices" tab to view the notice and respond to it (USCIS, Online Filing Options).

  • Answer through the RFE response, not the general "additional evidence" uploader. USCIS offers that uploader for evidence it did not ask for, "if you do not have an open RFE."
  • Each file must be 12MB or smaller and a PDF, JPG or JPEG. Do not encrypt or password-protect files.
  • For a document not in English, upload the original, the certified English translation and the translator's certificate.
  • Scan or photograph each page so every word is readable.

If you filed on paper, follow the response instructions printed on your RFE notice, and put the notice on top of your response. If your receipt number begins with "IOE" and you have the Online Access Code from your USCIS Account Access Notice, you can add the case to your USCIS online account and respond to the RFE there (USCIS, How to Create a USCIS Online Account).

  • Use the same legal names and dates as on your I-130 and I-130A.
  • Check every RFE item has at least one exhibit before you send.
  • Keep a complete copy of exactly what you sent.
  • If you mail it, use a tracked service and keep the proof of delivery. If you upload it, save the confirmation.

After you respond

  • Approval (NOA2): the case moves to NVC.
  • Notice of Intent to Deny (NOID): USCIS explains why it plans to deny. You get no more than 30 days to answer (8 CFR 103.2(b)(8)(iv)). Get an attorney's help with a NOID.
  • Interview: USCIS can call the petitioner to a field office in the United States when a response still leaves the relationship unproven. See the petitioner interview.
  • Denial: you can appeal or file a new I-130. The I-130 page gives the appeal deadline.
