Stage 2: National Visa Center (NVC)

⏱ Typical review 14 days if no issues $325 visa fee + $120 Affidavit of Support fee Reviewed 27 Sept 2026

Bottom line

You are here: USCIS has approved your I-130 and passed your case to the National Visa Center (NVC).

What happens: NVC collects your fees, the DS-260 application, and your financial and civil documents. The goal is Documentarily Qualified (DQ) status. DQ is the NVC minimum. London expects more, so meet the higher standard.

Do this first: Get the IRS tax transcript itself for every sponsor.

Action list

  1. Petitioner: Pay the $325 visa processing fee and the $120 Affidavit of Support fee from a US bank account, one after the other.
  2. Petitioner: Fill in the I-864 and sign it by hand. Line up a joint sponsor if you rely on assets, or if income is anywhere near the threshold.
  3. Petitioner: Get the IRS Tax Return Transcript for every sponsor and joint sponsor, and upload it before the interview.
  4. Beneficiary: Fill in the DS-260 from the worksheet, and print the confirmation page.
  5. Beneficiary: Order police certificates near the end of the I-130 wait or early at NVC. NVC needs them before DQ. Start with any country of nationality you lived in for more than 6 months but no longer live in.
  6. Either: Upload color scans of every page, then select Submit Documents. Uploading alone does not put you in the review queue.

Documents for this stage

  • NVC document checklist — the full submission: financial and civil documents, upload standards and failure points
  • I-864 worksheet — prepare every Affidavit of Support answer before you open CEAC; it does the sums for you
  • DS-260 worksheet — prepare every DS-260 answer, so a CEAC timeout does not lose your work
  • I-864 household calculator — your household size and minimum income threshold

Once USCIS approves the I-130, your case moves to the National Visa Center (NVC). NVC collects your fees, the DS-260 visa application, and your financial and civil documents. It then passes the case to the US Embassy London to schedule an interview. Your goal at this stage is Documentarily Qualified (DQ) status.

NVC's bar is lower than London's. Meet London's.

Documentarily Qualified (DQ) means NVC accepts that your documents meet the federal minimum. It does not mean the US Embassy London will accept the same evidence at interview. London looks harder at the same documents. Cases have reached DQ with, say, only a copy of the tax return, then been refused under 221(g) at interview because London wanted the IRS transcript itself.

This site always recommends London's higher standard, even where it goes beyond what NVC or the State Department officially requires. The interview is the finish line, not DQ. A 221(g) for a document you could have sent at NVC costs far more time than meeting the higher bar first. Where our checklists ask for more than USCIS or the State Department, we mean it: the extra reflects what London does.

The community Discord NVC channels give full step-by-step help. They follow the official NVC process at Travel.State.gov.

The most common RFEs (Requests for Evidence)

NVC does not use the term RFE. The community calls it an RFE when NVC rejects a document in CEAC and asks for it again.

  • Missing or incomplete Form I-864 (Affidavit of Support): The petitioner (main sponsor) and each joint sponsor fill in separate I-864s. Household members fill in I-864As.
  • Incomplete document scans: A folded corner or a missing side triggers an RFE. Show every page in full, and scan both sides of two-sided documents.
  • Inconsistent information between forms: Names, birthdates or addresses that differ between the I-130, I-864, DS-260 and civil documents cause RFEs. Use the same exact legal names everywhere.
  • Police certificates missing or expired: Certificates must be originals or certified copies. The UK ACRO certificate must be dated within 12 months of the interview. You need certificates from:
    • your current country of residence
    • your country of nationality, if you lived there more than 6 months
    • any other country you lived in for 12+ months after age 16
    • any country where you were arrested
    Citizenship alone, without living there, does not mean you need one.
  • An I-864 signed digitally instead of by hand.
  • Documents uploaded to the wrong CEAC category.
Prevention is faster than correction

Prepare your documents to the higher standard the US Embassy London uses, so your submission is complete the first time and leaves nothing to question.

Getting started

Usually 1–7 days after I-130 approval. Now and then it takes 2–3 weeks, which is nothing to worry about. NVC emails the petitioner and beneficiary from National_Visa_Center@state.gov with the subject Notice of Immigrant Visa Case Creation. The email contains your NVC Case Number (3 letters + 10 digits) and Invoice ID Number (IVSCA + 11 digits). Save both: you need them to sign in to CEAC.

Sign in at ceac.state.gov/IV with your Case Number and Invoice ID. The account can take up to 72 hours to work after the welcome letter. In CEAC you pay fees, submit the DS-260, upload documents and get messages from NVC.

12-month inactivity rule

INA 203(g) makes NVC close a case if the applicant does not apply for the visa within one year of hearing it is available. Only a real step on the case resets the clock: paying fees, submitting the DS-260 or documents, or contacting NVC. NVC may not count signing in to CEAC as contact, so do not rely on it. Closing the case also cancels the approved I-130.

Payments and application

FeeAmount
Immigrant Visa (IV) Application Processing Fee$325
Affidavit of Support Fee$120

Pay the two fees separately in CEAC, each from a US bank account (checking or savings). Both must clear before you can fill in the rest of CEAC, including the DS-260. Allow up to 10 calendar days for each. More info: NVC fee guidance.

The petitioner's income must reach 125% of the US Federal Poverty Guidelines for their household size. If it falls short, you need a joint sponsor. A petitioner living abroad should not assume their income counts. Income from a foreign job generally counts only if it appears on the US tax return and will continue after the move to the US. A foreign job usually will not continue. Use the NVC Poverty Guidelines Calculator to check.

Sign the I-864 by hand: print it, sign it in ink, and scan it. Do not use a digital or e-signature. The I-864EZ exists for simple cases, but many people have problems with it. We recommend the standard I-864 for everyone.

Petitioner: Relying on assets is risky at London

London is highly skeptical of petitioners who rely only on assets. Cases resting mainly on savings, property or investments have drawn a 221(g) at interview even when they met the asset rules, and often ended up needing a joint sponsor anyway. If you rely only on assets, get a joint sponsor. The same discretion applies to income. Officers have judged sponsor income "too low" at the window and asked for a joint sponsor even where it exceeded the 125% guideline. If income is near the threshold, line up a joint sponsor in advance.

Petitioner: I-864 preparation worksheet

Prepare every answer on the worksheet before you open CEAC. The form is long and CEAC is unreliable, so a finished worksheet saves you from losing work.

Petitioner: Finding your USCIS Online Account Number

The I-864 asks for your USCIS Online Account Number "(if any)". This 12-digit number is not your IOE receipt number, and myUSCIS does not show it on your profile. If you filed the I-130 on paper and never got a USCIS Account Access Notice, you may not have one. Leave the field blank. A blank answer does not make your case incomplete.

If your account shows a case with an IOE receipt number, you can make myUSCIS display the number by opening a new online I-130 draft. You enter nothing and submit nothing.

  1. Log in at my.uscis.gov. Under My Account → Home, check that Your Cases lists a receipt number starting with IOE.
  2. Go to My Account → File a form online.
  3. In Select the form you want to file online, choose I-130, Petition for Alien Relative and click Continue.
  4. Select Fill Out Form Field Online.
  5. Scroll down and click Next, then scroll down again and click Start.
  6. Select Next through the next six or so pages, without typing anything. Stop at the Additional information page, which opens with "What is your current immigration status?"
  7. Scroll to What is your USCIS Online Account Number? myUSCIS fills in the number for you. Copy it into the I-864.

Do not submit the draft or pay a fee. USCIS does not publish this route. A member of r/immigration shared it, and USCIS can change the screens at any time. For the form's own wording, see the I-864 instructions on uscis.gov.

Documents and checks

Petitioner: IRS tax transcript: treat it as required for London

NVC accepts either an IRS tax transcript or a complete copy of the filed return for the most recent year, and only "strongly recommends" the transcript (travel.state.gov, Step 5). London has still issued 221(g)s for a missing transcript after DQ, with the full return, W-2 and pay stubs on file. Get the transcript from irs.gov/individuals/get-transcript for every sponsor and joint sponsor, and upload it before the interview.

If you cannot get the transcript yet:

  • Filed an extension (DOS-recognized): a copy of IRS Form 4868 or a signed statement confirming it, plus the year before's transcript or return.
  • Not required to file (DOS-recognized): a signed statement citing the IRS filing threshold, plus other evidence of income such as pay stubs, benefit statements or an employer letter.
  • Filed, but the IRS has not issued the transcript (no official substitute): upload the signed 1040, W-2s, pay stubs and the IRS e-file acceptance, and add a CEAC note that the transcript will follow before the interview. Some members also put "Where's My Refund" screenshots and the acceptance email into one labelled PDF. That helps before the interview, but it has not cleared a 221(g) once issued. Only the transcript does that.

On 21 October 2026 London approved Matt without his joint sponsor's 2025 transcript. He had the sponsor's 2025 return, the extension to file, and returns and transcripts for the two years before, and the officer did not ask about it (Matt's account).

NVC is where you plan against a 221(g)

The best step you can take to avoid a 221(g) at interview: upload the IRS transcript itself for every sponsor here, and line up a joint sponsor if income is anywhere near the threshold. Our 221(g) page explains what to put in place now, before you DQ.

Plan against a 221(g) →
  • IRS Tax Return Transcript for the latest year, from IRS.gov. Create an account through ID.me if you do not have one. Use the Tax Return Transcript, which the IRS has accepted and verified. The form needs only the latest year, but we strongly recommend the last 3.
  • Credit check (new in the 08/24/26 edition of Form I-864): every sponsor now lets USCIS and the State Department pull their credit reports and scores. Lift any credit freeze, or be ready to lift it fast when asked. Check your own reports before you sign. See what the I-864 does and does not settle.
  • W-2s / 1099s
  • Proof of current employment: employment letter and recent pay stubs
  • Joint sponsor: a separate I-864, their own tax transcript, W-2s, and proof of US status and domicile. See "Joint sponsor: documents for each situation" below.
  • Evidence of US domicile for the petitioner, even if NVC does not list it, and for any joint sponsor. Upload it under "Other": driver's license, job offer, lease, US bills, school enrollment, moving quote, or a letter of intent. A joint sponsor can fix low income but cannot fix the petitioner's domicile. A lease alone shows an address, not where you live. See the petitioner's domicile problem
Petitioner: Self-employed sponsors

Required (USCIS I-864 checklist):

  • The complete federal tax return with every schedule attached: Schedule C (sole proprietor / single-member LLC), Schedule E (rental / partnership / S-corp), Schedule F (farm income), Schedule SE
  • The latest tax year is required; we strongly recommend up to 3 years
  • Any 1099s issued to or by the business

Recommended (makes the case stronger and an RFE or 221(g) less likely):

  • 12 months of business bank statements
  • A signed, dated statement describing the business and the sponsor's role and share of ownership
  • Business registration or EIN letter, and any licence the occupation requires
  • A signed year-to-date profit and loss statement
  • Client contracts or invoices showing ongoing work

None of these replaces the tax return. Officers treat Schedule C as the main evidence. Self-employed joint sponsors need the same.

Every joint sponsor needs:

  • Form I-864, separate from the petitioner's and signed by hand
  • IRS tax return transcript (see the transcript box under Step 5: treat it as required for London)
  • Proof of US citizenship or LPR status
  • Proof of US domicile

W-2 employed:

  • W-2s for the latest tax year (3 years recommended)
  • Employment letter on company letterhead (position, start date, salary)
  • Recent pay stubs (minimum 3, ideally 6 months)

Self-employed, or not required to file taxes: the same evidence as for the petitioner under Step 5 above.

Retired:

  • Social Security benefit statement (SSA-1099) or pension statement
  • Proof of ongoing benefit (award letter, most recent 1099-R)
  • If drawing from retirement accounts: statements showing distribution pattern

Military:

  • LES (Leave and Earnings Statement) showing base pay, BAH, BAS
  • Most recent W-2

Using a household member's income (I-864A):

  • Form I-864A completed by the household member
  • Household member's proof of income (same as their employment type above)
  • Proof the household member resides with the joint sponsor
  • Proof of relationship or lawful basis for including them

Using assets instead of, or alongside, income:

  • London has refused cases resting on assets even where the multiplier was met: 3× the shortfall for a US citizen's spouse or child aged 18 or over, 5× in most other cases. Get a joint sponsor instead.
  • If you use assets anyway: proof of ownership, acquisition date and value, and any liens against them.

If the sponsor is married and files jointly with a spouse who is not sponsoring:

  • The full joint return is fine, but the sponsor should also send W-2s or 1099s that show their own income. Only the sponsor's own income counts, unless the spouse also signs Form I-864A.

The DS-260 is the immigrant visa application form. You fill it in on CEAC. [CEAC DS-260] CEAC times out after about 10 minutes of inactivity, and you lose your work. Prepare every answer on the worksheet before you sign in. Once you submit, you cannot edit it yourself, so check everything twice. If you find an error, ask NVC to reopen it through the Public Inquiry Form, or tell the consular officer at the interview, who can correct it there.

You need: passport details, every address since age 16, 10 years of jobs, 5 years of travel, family details, and US contact details. Print the DS-260 confirmation page after you submit. You need it at the medical and the interview.

On the vaccination question: if you plan to get missing vaccines at the medical, choose "Other" and explain in the box.

For a child who is a US citizen (through a CRBA, for example), answer No to "Is the child immigrating with you?" The child returns as a citizen; they are not immigrating.

Final steps at NVC

Use this to work out which police certificates you need. You may need more than one. Each must be an original or a certified copy; NVC does not accept photocopies. [travel.state.gov: Civil Documents]

Beneficiary: Citizen of another country? Act early

If you are a citizen of a country you no longer live in, and you lived there for more than 6 months at any time, even as a child, you need a police certificate from that country. Holding the passport without ever living there does not mean you need one. The embassy will not issue a visa until you meet this requirement.

The UK ACRO certificate lasts 12 months. Certificates from other countries generally never expire, as long as you have not gone back to live there. Order early anyway, because processing times vary widely by country.

A police certificate from a country you do not live in now can take months, and you may have to go there in person. Find out early what you need, and start near the end of the I-130 wait or early at NVC. NVC needs every certificate before it grants DQ. The UK ACRO certificate takes about 30 days.

If you… And… You need a certificate from…
Hold citizenship of any country You are 16 or older, and lived there for more than 6 months at any point in your life, including as a child That country, even if you left long ago
Are 16 or older Currently living in a country different from your nationality, for more than 6 months Your current country of residence
Have ever lived in another country for 12+ months Were 16 or older when you lived there That country
Were ever arrested anywhere Whatever your age, the length of stay or the outcome The city and/or country where you were arrested

Note: People who live or lived in the US do not need a US police certificate.

Validity (London embassy guidance): The UK police certificate (ACRO) lasts 12 months from the date of issue. Every other police certificate lasts indefinitely, unless you go back and live in that country for 6+ months (your country of birth or current residence) or 12+ months (any other country). Source: US Embassy London FAQ

  • Birth certificate (long form with parent names)
  • Marriage certificate
  • Divorce or death certificates for every earlier marriage, if any
  • UK ACRO police certificate: required for every applicant aged 16 or over. The embassy accepts it for 12 months, whatever shorter validity it shows.
  • Foreign police certificates: one for every country you lived in for 12+ months after age 16, every country of nationality you lived in for more than 6 months, and every country where you were arrested. The decision tree above confirms what you need.
  • Passport biographic page
  • Court, prison, or military records (if applicable)

Documents not in English need certified translations. Check the NVC Document Finder / Reciprocity Schedule for each country's rules. Scan in color, include every page, and keep each file under 2 MB (CEAC's limit); PDF is best. For certified translations, members recommend Rush Translate.

After you upload everything, select Submit Documents. Uploading alone does not put you in the review queue. Do not post originals to NVC. Bring them to the interview.

Once you submit, NVC reviews your case. A review usually takes 14 days if nothing is wrong.

Two possible outcomes:

  • RFE (Request for Evidence): fix the problem, upload again and resubmit. The 10 business day clock starts again each time you resubmit.
  • DQ (Documentarily Qualified): NVC accepts every document, and your case joins the embassy queue. CEAC still shows "At NVC" until your interview letter arrives.

Note: DQ does not mean your evidence is enough. It only puts you in the queue for an interview. London often expects more at interview than NVC does for DQ. Meet the higher bar the first time where you can, to avoid delay.

What DQ does not mean
  • that your interview is scheduled
  • that CEAC shows your case at the embassy: it still says "At NVC"
  • that you will get a visa
  • that nobody will ask for more documents

What the DQ notice email looks like

The DQ notice arrives by email from National_Visa_Center@state.gov with the subject Notice regarding your Immigrant Visa Case becoming Documentarily Qualified. The name and Case ID below are placeholders. Read what it says and what it does not. NVC has everything it needs and will now work with London on scheduling. Your interview is not scheduled yet. The date comes later, in a separate notice (see Stage 3).

Expect this wording to change

NVC can change this notice at any time, and we expect it to change with the coming public charge changes, above all the line saying sponsors may need to give updated evidence of other income, assets and current employment. The text below is the notice as NVC sent it when we wrote this, not a fixed template. Read it with the public charge section.

From: National Visa Center <National_Visa_Center@state.gov>
To: [Beneficiary] / [Petitioner]
Subject: Notice regarding your Immigrant Visa Case becoming Documentarily Qualified

Dear BENEFICIARY,

The National Visa Center (NVC) received all of the fees, forms, and documents that are required prior to scheduling your immigrant visa interview at the U.S. Embassy/Consulate General overseas.

NVC will work with the U.S. Embassy/Consulate General in LONDON, GRBR to schedule an interview appointment for you. Once we have confirmed an interview date, we will send a notice to you, your petitioner, and attorney (if applicable).

Please do NOT make any travel arrangements, sell property, or give up employment until you have received an immigrant visa from the U.S. Embassy/Consulate General.

The U.S. Embassy/Consulate General may require additional documentation at the time of the interview. Please visit https://nvc.state.gov/prep for information about immigrant visa interviews. Additionally, the petitioner, as the sponsor, and all other sponsors (household members and joint sponsors), may be required to provide updated or additional evidence to you to present at the time of your interview, such as:

  • Other income
  • Assets
  • Proof of current employment

Note: You submitted documentation to the National Visa Center through the Consular Electronic Application Center (CEAC). You can continue to upload documents to CEAC up until the date of your visa interview.

Regards,
National Visa Center,
U.S. Department of State

Case ID: LND0123456789

**NOTE: Please do not reply to this email. This is not a monitored account. If you have questions, please review the help topics available to you at https://nvc.state.gov/ask.**

If you have not heard from NVC within 10 business days, send a Public Inquiry on the NVC website.

Family members with different DQ dates: act now

If several family members apply together, such as a spouse and children who could not get a CRBA, or stepchildren, they may reach DQ at different times and get different interview dates.

Email the embassy and ask it to put every case in the same interview appointment. Include the IOE numbers (case reference numbers) for every applicant. The embassy can group them so everyone is seen on the same day.

Email: LNDIVSubmissions@state.gov

Do this as soon as you see the DQ dates differ. Do not wait until separate ILs have gone out.

Choosing when to finish NVC

Some applicants hold off on paying NVC fees or submitting documents, to control when their case joins the London queue. They may want a better time of year for the interview, to match a job start date, or to avoid disrupting a school year.

That is a fair personal choice, and NVC does not penalize a slower case. The London queue may be longer or shorter by the time you DQ, and nobody can predict it more than a few months ahead.

If you do hold at NVC, look at the Visa Bulletin and any known backlogs when you decide. Community data on the tracker shows where the London queue stands now.

12-month inactivity rule: INA §203(g)

If you do not apply within one year of NVC telling you a visa is available, NVC closes the case under INA 203(g), and the I-130 approval is cancelled automatically (8 CFR 205.1(a)(1)). To reopen it, you must prove within two years that the delay was beyond your control. Only a real step on the case resets the clock: paying fees, submitting the DS-260 or documents, or contacting NVC. NVC may not count signing in to CEAC as contact, so do not rely on it. If you are holding your case on purpose, take a real step well inside every 12 months.
