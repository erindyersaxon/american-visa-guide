Stage 1A: Delayed I-130

Consider escalating at 12+ months after your priority date Demand letter at 15–18 months; writ 30 days after USCIS receives it Reviewed 27 Sept 2026

Bottom line

Stage 1A: Delayed I-130. Your I-130 has been pending significantly longer than typical processing times, generally 12+ months after your priority date. Escalation can encourage USCIS to act, in order: USCIS case inquiry, congressional inquiry, demand letter, then writ of mandamus. Only the writ is a legal remedy; the inquiries are requests for help. A writ can compel a decision, not an approval. Do this first: at 12+ months, start a congressional inquiry, and consult an AILA immigration attorney before any legal action.

Action list

  1. Petitioner: At 12+ months pending, submit a USCIS case inquiry, then a congressional inquiry through one office. USCIS will discuss the petition only with the petitioner or the attorney on a Form G-28, not the beneficiary. A White House contact form is optional and informal.
  2. Petitioner: Document every remedy you initiate. Send a demand letter at 15–18 months, once other remedies have been initiated and documented. If USCIS has not decided 30 days after it receives the letter, a writ of mandamus is the next step.
  3. Petitioner: Before a demand letter or writ of mandamus, consult an immigration attorney affiliated with the American Immigration Lawyers Association (AILA).
  4. Either: Read the remedies guide for how each step works before you start.

Documents for this stage

  • Delayed I-130 remedies guide — each escalation step in order, from case inquiry and congressional inquiry to demand letter and writ of mandamus
  • Filing a writ of mandamus yourself — the steps for a self-represented filing, checked against the Federal Rules

If your I-130 petition has been pending significantly longer than typical processing times: generally 12+ months after your priority date without approval. A demand letter has its best chance at 15–18 months, and a writ follows 30 days after USCIS receives an unanswered letter. There are legal remedies available to encourage USCIS action. This section outlines those options in the order you might pursue them.

Why this stage now applies to almost everyone filing consular

Consular I-130 adjudication stalled in February 2026. The front of the line has not moved meaningfully past priority dates of around 25 February 2025 since, so anyone at or behind that line is already 19+ months pending: past both markers below, without ever being an outlier. The month thresholds on this page were written for a moving queue. They still describe when escalation is worth attempting, but they no longer distinguish a delayed case from an ordinary one. See Stage 1: I-130 for the current status, and note that escalation compels a decision at best, never an approval.

What escalation can and cannot do

A writ of mandamus is the only step that can compel USCIS to act, and it compels a decision, not an approval. Courts weigh the six TRAC factors, and there is no month count in the law. A demand letter does not cause a denial: if USCIS denies after one, the merits of the case caused it.

Forcing a decision can surface a problem in the file, such as a criminal record or a background check concern. Consult an attorney affiliated with the American Immigration Lawyers Association (AILA) before a demand letter or a writ.

Timeline & Milestones
  • 12+ months: Submit a USCIS case inquiry, then a congressional inquiry through one office.
  • 15–18 months: Send a demand letter, once the earlier steps are documented.
  • 30 days after USCIS receives the demand letter: If USCIS has not decided, file a writ of mandamus.
