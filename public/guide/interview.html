Stage 5: Embassy Interview

⏱ Interview 20–40 minutes; 1.5–4.5 hours in the embassy Arrive 30–45 minutes before your appointment South Pavilion, 33 Nine Elms Lane, London SW11 7US Reviewed 23 Sept 2026

Bottom line

Stage 5: Embassy interview. Your medical and document check are done and you have an interview date. By interview day most of the paperwork is done, but the officer still makes their own public charge assessment, now using a structured worksheet in every case. Answers must match your DS-260 and CEAC documents exactly. A decision or next step is typically communicated on the day. Do this first: make sure every financial document is already in CEAC, and send a public charge statement at least five working days ahead.

Action list

  1. Either: Make sure all financial evidence is already uploaded to CEAC. Unuploaded financial documents will not be accepted at the interview.
  2. Beneficiary: Pack your passport, the sealed document packet from the document check, and the courier registration confirmation.
  3. Beneficiary: Bring originals of everything else, and a paper copy of what is in CEAC in case the embassy cannot see an upload.
  4. Beneficiary: Arrive 30–45 minutes early, go to the South Pavilion off Ponton Road, and tell staff: “I am here for an immigrant visa interview.”
  5. Beneficiary: Rehearse your answers against your DS-260. They must match it exactly.

US Embassy London: 33 Nine Elms Lane, London SW11 7US
Nearest stations: Vauxhall or Nine Elms. Common nearby hotel: Travelodge Vauxhall.

What officers are actually assessing

By interview day, USCIS has approved the petition, NVC has accepted your documents, civil documents are verified, and the medical is done. None of that settles the one question the officer must still decide for themselves: whether you are likely to become a public charge. NVC checks that the paperwork is complete, not that the finances are sufficient, and the officer is not bound by that review.

Public charge: what has changed
  • November 2025: officers began weighing the five statutory factors (age, health, family status, assets and finances, education and skills) about you, the beneficiary. A sufficient I-864 no longer ends the inquiry on its own. This rests on practitioner analysis of an unpublished cable.
  • 15 August 2026: a structured public charge worksheet was deployed to officers at every post.
  • 1 September 2026: mandatory public charge training and expanded managerial review of immigrant visa decisions took effect worldwide.
  • 10 September 2026: cable 26 STATE 88862 told all posts to complete the worksheet in every immigrant visa case.

The worksheet, training and review come from the government's own court filings in CLINIC v. Rubio (S.D.N.Y.) and Medani v. Trump (N.D. Cal.). Sources are listed on the public charge page.

What this means in the room. Expect questions about your own work, savings, health and qualifications, not only your sponsor's income. The officer may also ask about the petitioner's age, dependents and debts. The worksheet is filled in for every case, so a short interview is still possible, but prepare as though it will not be. One London account from 2026 describes a 35–40 minute interview. The officer had read the applicant's public charge statement and medical report, checked with a supervisor throughout, and asked to see savings statements and proof of property at the window. That is a single case, not a pattern: London interviews under the new standard were largely cancelled through 30 September 2026 for the training, so there is little London experience to go on yet.

Before your interview: send a public charge statement covering all five factors, uploaded to CEAC and emailed to the unit at least five working days ahead. Bring printed proof of every asset you name, and know your figures in US dollars. The public charge page has the template, the questions posts are asking, and the documents a public charge 221(g) asks for.

A decision or next step is usually communicated on the day. If your passport is kept and you are told they intend to approve your visa, that is good news. If the officer is not yet satisfied on public charge, the usual result is a 221(g) listing what to provide, not an outright refusal. Some cases are placed into administrative processing, and you will be told what is needed to resolve it.

US Embassy London: 33 Nine Elms Lane, London SW11 7US

Arrival

Arrive 30–45 minutes before your scheduled appointment. Go directly to the staff member at the front and state clearly: "I am here for an immigrant visa interview." This is still the right first move, but be aware that in practice the queue situation outside can be chaotic, particularly at early morning slots.

The outside queue: what to expect

One community member's experience from June 2026 indicates that staff at the entrance directed all applicants into a single queue regardless of visa type, at least until the building opened. This may not reflect the norm: other members have gone straight in on stating they had an immigrant visa interview. If you go to the front and identify yourself as an immigrant visa applicant, you may still be told to join the queue.

Recommended approach: Go to the front as soon as you arrive and make clear you have an immigrant visa interview. If staff direct you to join the queue, do so immediately. Do not hang back. Being compliant but early in the queue is better than hesitating and ending up further back. Once you reach the front window inside, state that you are there for an immigrant visa interview and you will be directed straight in. Everything after the entrance is smooth and efficient.

If you are with someone, it may help to have them hold your place in the queue while you check with staff whether immigrant visa applicants have a separate line.

What to bring

Core documents
  • Passport, valid for at least six months beyond your intended date of entry into the US
  • Sealed document packet (received at document check)
  • Courier registration confirmation
No document check? Bring every original

Same-day document checks were canceled from late August 2026, so many applicants now reach the interview without one and without a sealed packet. London's interview pack for these cases, emailed with the interview notice, says originals must be presented at the interview and that failing to bring all required documents may result in your appointment being canceled. It lists:

  • Valid passport, valid for six months beyond your intended date of entry into the US
  • One passport photo (normally handed in at the document check)
  • Birth certificate: UK birth certificates must show at least one parent's name
  • Divorce decree or death certificate for every previous marriage, yours and your spouse's
  • Evidence of assets and liabilities: savings, property, bank statements, the affidavit of support, or a US job offer
  • ACRO police certificate for every applicant aged 16 or over, plus foreign police certificates for any country of citizenship you have lived in, any country where you lived more than 12 months after age 16, and any country where you were arrested
  • If applicable: adoption certificate, deed poll or statutory declaration (not needed for a change of name through marriage), military records
  • Certified translations of anything not in English
  • Your courier confirmation page from AIS

Rescheduling only goes one way. The pack says you can cancel or reschedule through AIS, but only to a date after your assigned appointment. Before you move a short-notice slot, weigh that you cannot get an earlier one back. Diversity visa applicants receive a version of the pack that also covers the DV fee, payable at the interview by card or cash (US dollars or pounds, no contactless), and the 30 September 2026 issuance deadline.

If you had a document check, these are often the only documents physically reviewed, because most materials were pre-verified there. Bring originals of everything else (I-864, marriage certificate, ACRO, tax documents, DS-260 confirmation) but expect they may not be requested. Officers rely primarily on what is already uploaded to CEAC.

Why paper copies still matter

Occasionally a document uploaded to CEAC isn't visible on the embassy's side. A CR1 applicant in July 2026 was told at document check that staff could not see the sponsor's 2025 IRS tax transcript despite it being uploaded. The paper copy they had brought was taken and attached to the case on the spot, and the interview proceeded normally. This is different from unuploaded financial evidence (which won't be accepted): a paper copy of something already in CEAC can rescue a visibility glitch.

What not to bring
  • Laptops or large bags
  • Unuploaded financial documents. The officer will not accept them
  • "Backup" paperwork not already in CEAC

Financial issues are the dominant cause of 221(g) at London. If it is not already in CEAC before interview day, it is too late. Meeting NVC's minimum to reach DQ is not the same as meeting London's standard at interview; this guide's recommendations target the higher bar.

South Pavilion: where beneficiaries go for interview

The interview takes place at the South Pavilion, which is a separate building alongside Ponton Road on the south side of the embassy complex. Do not go to the main Nine Elms Lane entrance. Follow signs or ask staff to direct you to the South Pavilion Consular Services entrance.

South Pavilion is on the south side of the embassy complex, accessed via Ponton Road

Interview structure

1
Document window
Submit passport and sealed packet. Confirm courier details. Provide fingerprints if requested.
2
Interview window
Take oath. Answer questions. Expect 20–40 minutes: since the public charge worksheet was deployed to consular officers, the financial questioning is a sustained line of enquiry rather than a box-tick. Receive decision. Total time in embassy: 1.5–4.5 hours. Before the public charge worksheet, community members reported 30 minutes to 2 hours; the longer financial questioning has moved both ends of that.

Answers must match your DS-260 and CEAC documents exactly. The interview is a consistency check, not a new evaluation.

Community interview experiences

All reported outcomes below are approvals. The pattern is consistent: long wait, professional officers.

Titus: CR1, July 2026
Easy security check, checked in, received a queue number and waited. Only the sealed folder and passport were physically checked: everything else was already in CEAC. One hiccup: staff couldn't see the sponsor's 2025 IRS tax transcript on their side, but the paper copy brought along was taken at document check and attached to the case there and then. Questions after the oath: how and where did you meet, where do you both work, any kids, any previous marriages, where does your spouse live. Interview duration: 2 minutes. Approved. Their advice: "Don't be stressed if you have all the documents and just be yourself."
Keely: CR1, January 2026
Skipped queue, submitted sealed packet and passport within minutes. Waited ~1 hour. Questions: where does your husband live, how did you meet, what does he do for work. Interview duration: ~40 seconds. Approved.
Walsh: CR1 + IR2 (child), January 2026
Large queue outside, bypassed immediately on stating immigrant visa. First call to submit sealed packet and passports, interview ~30 minutes later. Total time: ~45 minutes. CR1 questions covered sponsorship, how they met, wedding, travel, job plans, and custody documents. IR2 child asked about name change, prior meetings, feelings about moving. Both approved.
Neri: IR1, March 2026
Bypassed queue on arrival. Note: initial ticket had an error and required two returns to reception before the correct ticket (name and case number) was issued. Check this carefully. Documents taken at ~9:10, interview called just before 9:30. Questions: where did you meet, how many times visited the US, joint sponsor details, husband's job and location. Total time: ~2 hours. Approved.
Queen B: IR1, July 2025
Arrived 7:40 for 8:00 appointment. Sent directly to window, sticker on passport, bypassed queue. Questions: how did you meet, prior marriages, residence history. Interview ~5 minutes. Total time inside: ~30 minutes. Approved: issued same day.
HT3: IR5 (parent of US citizen), December 2025
Did not join queue. Submitted sealed folder and passport. Questions: how did your son become a citizen, relationship to joint sponsor, light background discussion. Interview ~5 minutes. Total time: ~1.5 hours. Approved.

Common pattern across all cases: skip the main queue, state "immigrant visa," two-step process (document window then interview window), interview 1–10 minutes, decision immediate. Check your sticker has the correct name and case number. All six accounts above pre-date the public charge worksheet. Expect substantially longer now — see the timing note in Interview structure.

Esther: spouse visa, 2026 (after the public charge change)
Had submitted a public charge statement in advance. The officer had read it and the medical report, and consulted a supervisor throughout. Questions: why the sponsor’s 2025 income was slightly below the minimum (he had opened a new business) and what he would do if it failed, whether he is in a union and has a mortgage, who she works for and whether her employer would permit her to work from the US, her savings balance and proof of the property she owns (asked for at the window), cash income from a family business, and whether she knew what insulin costs in the US. Interview duration: 35–40 minutes. Approved. Read the full account on the public charge page →
