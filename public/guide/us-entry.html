Stage 9: US entry

⏱ Green card up to 90 days from entry or fee payment $235 USCIS Immigrant Fee Reviewed 27 Sept 2026

You are here: You have your visa and you are travelling.

What happens: You become a lawful permanent resident when a CBP officer inspects you and admits you on your immigrant visa. That happens at the port of entry: an airport, a preclearance airport, or a land border. The visa lets you ask to enter. It does not guarantee entry. USCIS mails your green card once you pay the $235 USCIS Immigrant Fee.

Do this first: Pay the Immigrant Fee, and confirm your US address with the CBP officer.

Action list

  1. Beneficiary: Pay the $235 USCIS Immigrant Fee at my.uscis.gov. USCIS will not make your green card until you pay.
  2. Beneficiary: At the port of entry, confirm or update your US address with the CBP officer. USCIS mails your green card there.
  3. Beneficiary: Sign up for USPS Informed Delivery as soon as you arrive.
  4. Beneficiary: If your SSN card has not arrived 10 business days after entry, go to your local SSA office. Take your passport with the visa stamp and your foreign birth certificate.
  5. Either: If you move, update your address with USCIS within 10 days (AR-11). Separately, while their I-864 obligation lasts, the petitioner and any joint sponsor must file Form I-865 within 30 days of their own move.

Documents for this stage

  • Life in the USA — green card, SSN, banking, tax, Selective Service, and where the community has settled

Arrived in the US? Add your date of entry to the US to the community tracker →

Show your passport with the immigrant visa at the port of entry. The CBP officer inspects you and decides whether to let you in. The visa does not guarantee entry. The officer can still find you inadmissible, for example if your facts have changed since the interview, or if something comes to light that you did not disclose. The moment the officer admits you, you are a lawful permanent resident.

At the port of entry

  • Confirm or update your US address with the CBP officer. USCIS mails your green card to this address.
  • London handles documents digitally. Your visa shows "IV Docs in CCD": CBP can see your documents electronically at the border. You do not need a sealed envelope. Some other embassies still give one out for the border. London does not.
  • If you have Global Entry, tell Global Entry in person that your status has changed. The easiest time is as you enter as a green card holder.
US Preclearance: clear CBP before you leave

Several airports outside the US have CBP preclearance. Clearing there counts as your official port of entry, and you land in the US as a domestic passenger, with no immigration queue. Immigrant visa holders can use all of them.

Drawbacks: once you are through, you find few shops and few backup flights, above all at Shannon. Irish preclearance keeps fixed hours. Flights outside those hours clear CBP when they land in the US instead.

Entering via Puerto Rico or US Territories

Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands, Guam, American Samoa and the Northern Mariana Islands are US territories. Immigrant visa holders can enter through San Juan (SJU) or another territory airport. You become a permanent resident there, just as at a mainland US airport.

If you settle in a territory rather than a state, some USCIS and SSA steps may differ slightly from the mainland.

Entering by land from Mexico or Canada

Immigrant visa holders can use any staffed Class A land port of entry on either border. You show your passport with the immigrant visa to the CBP officer, just as at an airport. Land crossings have no preclearance. Inspection happens at the US border, so expect to queue. Waits for walkers and cars can differ a lot. CBP reads London visas ("IV Docs in CCD") electronically at land borders too.

  • Busiest Mexico crossings: San Ysidro and Otay Mesa (Tijuana), Calexico, Nogales, El Paso, Laredo.
  • Busiest Canada crossings: Peace Arch and Pacific Highway (Blaine, WA), Detroit (Windsor), Port Huron, the Buffalo and Niagara Falls bridges, Champlain (NY).

Confirm your US mailing address with the officer, because USCIS sends the green card there.

Green card

USCIS starts making your green card (Form I-551) once you pay the $235 USCIS Immigrant Fee. Stage 7 covers how to pay. USCIS gives these delivery times:

Entry Fee timing When to expect the card
Entered on immigrant visa Fee paid before entry Up to 90 days from entry date
Entered on immigrant visa Fee paid after entry Up to 90 days from payment date
Entered on immigrant visa Fee not yet paid USCIS will not make the card until you pay at my.uscis.gov

Source: egov.uscis.gov: When to expect your Green Card

Beneficiary: Keep your address up to date

USCIS mails your green card to the address you gave at your visa interview, or the new one you gave at the port of entry. If you move after you arrive, update your address in the beneficiary's USCIS account, or by post on the AR-11, within 10 days. The law requires this. The petitioner and any joint sponsor have a separate duty: while their I-864 obligation lasts, each must file Form I-865, Sponsor's Notice of Change of Address, within 30 days of moving (INA 213A(d)). If they do not, they face civil fines. A US citizen sponsor does not file an AR-11.

One member updated their address, and USCIS still sent the card to the old one. The landlord forwarded it, a week late. After entry, check the address in your USCIS account and take a screenshot.

Beneficiary: Long names may print shortened

Some USCIS systems limit the number of characters, so a long name can appear cut short, or without hyphens, on your green card and other USCIS documents. This is normal, and you do not need to ask for a correction. When you apply for a driver's license or state ID, bring your birth or marriage certificate to show your full legal name. See USCIS Policy Manual, Vol. 11, Part A, Ch. 2, footnote 3.

Beneficiary: Carry proof of your status

Every permanent resident aged 18 or over must carry proof of registration at all times (INA 264(e), 8 U.S.C. § 1304(e)). Until your green card arrives, that is your passport with the immigrant visa stamp. After that, it is the green card. Not carrying it is a federal misdemeanour, and the government has pushed enforcement of the registration rules since 2025. A photocopy does not count.

Tracking the card

Your Immigrant Fee receipt has an IOE number for Form I-551. It is not your visa case number, and not the IOE number on your I-130. Enter it at egov.uscis.gov to track production.

Set up myUSCIS accounts before you need them: one for the beneficiary, and one each for the petitioner and any joint sponsor, linked to the case. In the beneficiary's account you can track the case and file an AR-11 address change online, which is faster than the paper form.

The USPS tracking number often does not appear in your USCIS online status. To get it, contact USCIS: use the Emma chat at uscis.gov, or call 1 (800) 375-5283 and ask for a live agent.

Biometrics appointment (I-797C)

Some members get an I-797C notice after entry, calling them to a biometrics appointment at an Application Support Center (ASC) before USCIS issues the green card. This is not standard. It happens when USCIS cannot use the biometrics it already has. Known causes: you held a green card before (old biometrics expire after 15 months), or the photo or fingerprints from the embassy were poor quality.

If you get this notice, bring the I-797C, valid photo ID, and your passport with the visa stamp. USCIS usually makes the card within days of the appointment. Do not skip it: USCIS will not make the card until you complete biometrics. The notice does not mean anything is wrong with your case or your permanent resident status.

The Service Center code on the notice (FCE means Florida, for example) shows the processing center, not the state you live in. The TCR field may be blank. That is normal.

Social Security Number

If you answered "Yes" to the SSN question on the DS-260 (Enumeration on Entry), the Social Security Administration (SSA) starts your application automatically and mails the card after you arrive. You need no appointment. [SSA: SSN for Immigrants] [SSA: Enumeration on Entry FAQ] You cannot track the card once it is in the mail.

If your card has not arrived 10 business days after entry, go to your local SSA office. Several members ticked the option on the DS-260, and the SSA still had no record of their application. Staff took a new application on the spot. Bring your passport with the visa stamp and your foreign birth certificate. Some offices serve only people who live in their area, so check with your local office before you go. Some people book an appointment in advance, in case the card does not come.

SSA appointment tip

If the SSA officer does not know immigrant visas, or asks for an I-94, the SSA's own policy manual explains how to handle immigrant visa holders. A printout can help: secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0110211025

Some offices take walk-ins. Check Google reviews for your local office before you go. Make or change an appointment here.

Common problems: the card goes to the wrong address, the SSA misses the apartment number, or the application never starts despite the DS-260 answer. Confirm your full address, including the apartment number, with SSA staff.

Register for USPS Informed Delivery

USPS Informed Delivery shows you scans of your mail before it arrives. Sign up as soon as you arrive, and you will see the green card and SSN card coming before they reach your mailbox.
