Stage 3: Waiting for your interview letter

⏱ DQ to IL averaged 77 days in the six months to July 2026 No drop since 14 July 2026 IL to interview ~2 months Reviewed 27 Sept 2026

Bottom line

Stage 3: Waiting for your interview letter. You are Documentarily Qualified and in the embassy scheduling queue; CEAC will still show “At NVC” until your Interview Letter arrives. Drops have stopped since 14 July 2026, so this is your best opportunity to get organized. Do this first: write your public charge statement, and check your tax documents are current.

Action list

  1. Beneficiary: Write the public charge statement, in consultation with your petitioner, and build the evidence pack behind it. Upload both to your case in CEAC, then email them to LNDIVSubmissions@state.gov.
  2. Petitioner: If the US tax filing deadline has passed since you reached DQ, upload the most recent tax transcript to CEAC. The steps are in Stage 4.
  3. Beneficiary: Check your ACRO certificate and passport will still be valid: the passport for at least 6 months beyond your intended US entry date.
  4. Beneficiary: Check every police certificate you need is in hand and in date, including any from a country of nationality you no longer live in but lived in for more than 6 months. Australia’s takes up to two months.
  5. Beneficiary: Arrange health insurance to bridge the gap before arrival, and keep the quote or certificate for your statement.
  6. Beneficiary: When the IL arrives, go to Stage 4: Interview letter received.

After DQ, your case is complete at NVC and enters the embassy scheduling queue. This stage is a waiting and preparation phase. Your CEAC status will still show "At NVC" until your Interview Letter arrives.

Wait Times

Wait times from DQ to Interview Letter vary and change over time. See the data page for current community averages.

How IL drops work

The embassy schedules interviews in batches, not continuously. Batches are not on a fixed schedule, but until mid-2026 they arrived at least once a month, and occasionally more than once. Whether you receive an IL in a given batch depends on your DQ date relative to others in the queue. No batch has been reported since 14 July 2026. See below.

What 16 months of drops tell us

Based on 25 drops tracked by this community, March 2025 – July 2026. Updated 3 September 2026.

  • A monthly rhythm. One main batch every 4 to 5 weeks, with small top-ups between. Each main batch fills about one calendar month of interview slots.
  • Weekday afternoons. See when ILs arrive.
  • Mostly first come, first served. Every third or fourth batch is a wider catch-up sweep that picks up people waiting longer than average. If you feel skipped, expect to be picked up in one of those.
  • A longer wait from DQ to IL. It averaged 77 days over the six months to July 2026, up from 63 all-time.
  • IL to interview was two calendar months, without exception up to July 2026. Interviews scheduled that way are now being cancelled: see the cancellation notice.

Right now: drops have stopped. The last, small one came on 14 July 2026, and the gap since is longer than any in the 16 months tracked. Members at other posts report the same. Read this card as history, not a forecast.

This data reflects Immediate Relative (IR) visas

Nearly all members hold Immediate Relative (IR) visas, so the drop figures describe IR cases. Family-Sponsored Preference (F) cases run about a month behind: on 8 July 2026 the State Department's NVC scheduling status tool showed London scheduling IR cases from April 2026 and F cases from March 2026. Use that tool for official figures; the tracker counts only members who submit.

When do ILs arrive?

Based on observed London channel data, ILs arrive by email from National_Visa_Center@state.gov with the subject Immigrant Visa Interview Appointment, always on a weekday, typically early-to-mid afternoon UK time (around 13:50–16:00). Fridays are most common, followed by Wednesdays and Mondays, no drop has ever arrived on a weekend. A CEAC status update follows: it will show your case as being at the embassy.

Drop date, day, and time data is kept up to date on the data page: see the IL Drop History table there.

Use the waiting time to prepare

This period is your best opportunity to get organized before things accelerate. Make sure all of these are current and start to organize your binder.

Documents & admin
ACRO police certificate
Valid 12 months for embassy purposes.
Standard: £68, about 30 days  ·  Priority (2 working days): suspended
ACRO has suspended its 2-day priority service, so you cannot buy your way out of a late order. Allow the full standard time. If the certificate you uploaded at NVC will be more than 12 months old on your interview date, order a new one no later than the day your Interview Letter arrives.
acro.police.uk →
Other police certificates
Check you hold every certificate you need, and that each is still in date for your interview. That includes any country of nationality you no longer live in but lived in for more than 6 months, even if that was as a child. These are the ones that get missed, and they are usually the slowest to obtain from the UK. Not sure which you need? Use the police certificate decision tree in Stage 2.
Australia (AFP National Police Check): allow two months. The AFP accepts only ink fingerprints on paper, with full palm and side prints, and few UK providers still take them. Phone the AFP to confirm the form. Tracked post shows nothing until the envelope reaches Australia, and the fee leaving your account is your confirmation. Members reported just under a month and 57 days, start to finish.
afp.gov.au: National Police Checks →
Civil document originals
Confirm you have originals accessible: marriage certificate, birth certificate, divorce decrees. Check nothing is expiring or needs replacement.
I-864 financial documents
Recent pay stubs · Employment letter confirming salary · IRS tax transcripts (up to 3 years) · W-2s. If using a joint sponsor, ensure they have the same documents.
If sponsor or joint sponsor is self-employed: updated 12-month business bank statements and a current year-to-date profit and loss statement, since tax returns filed at NVC stage may now be over a year old.
Passport expiry
Must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your intended US entry date. Apply for renewal now if needed.

Medical preparation: check your vaccination records against the CDC list now, and gather reports for any significant condition. Request the GP Summary Care Record later, 2 to 3 weeks before the medical. Stage 5 covers each.

Use this prep time to head off a 221(g)

The most common reason a London case stalls at the window is a financial gap that was fixable during exactly this waiting period: a missing IRS transcript, income near the threshold with no joint sponsor lined up, or out-of-date tax documents. Our 221(g) page sets out the planned mitigations to lock down now, before your interview.

Public Charge: prepare your written statement

The officer now tests public charge in every case, and a sufficient I-864 no longer settles it. The officer weighs five factors about you, the beneficiary: age, health, family status, assets and resources, and education and skills. You carry the burden of proof. Use the waiting time to get this done:

  • Write the statement. The statement builder takes about 30 minutes. Answer "how will this be paid for" with recurring income in every section, and put savings last as a buffer. London does not let assets stand in for income.
  • Gather the evidence. The last 3 years of IRS tax returns for every sponsor, the last 3 months of payslips and bank statements, proof of the petitioner's health insurance, and your UK student loan statement if you have one.
  • Send it at least five working days before the interview. Upload it to CEAC first, then email LNDIVSubmissions@state.gov so the unit knows it is there. Bring a printed copy.
  • Rehearse the answers out loud. The officer asks you, not your sponsor. Learn the petitioner's income, debts and insurance as well as your own.

Health insurance: the coverage gap

There are two periods to plan for:

Before you arrive. Your US health insurance options typically don't begin until you are resident in the US and able to enroll. Short-term international health insurance can bridge this gap. Community members have used:

  • Cigna Global: offers an online quote in a few minutes at cignaglobal.com. No commitment to purchase. The quote summary is a downloadable PDF you can include with your public charge statement as evidence that you have researched coverage.
  • IMG Patriot America Plus: community members have obtained 6 months of coverage without needing a US SSN. IMG will issue a certificate of coverage, which can be included in your public charge statement.

These are community-reported examples only and are not recommendations. Research plans to find what suits your situation.

After you arrive. Once you are a permanent resident living in the US, you are eligible to enroll through the ACA marketplace at healthcare.gov. Plans are available at a range of price points. Premium tax credits exist for lower incomes, but the enhanced credits expired at the end of 2025 and eligibility rules for immigrants were narrowed by the 2025 budget law (permanent residents remain eligible), so price the unsubsidized premium. For your public charge statement, show how coverage will be paid from income, not from a subsidy. You can browse plans before you arrive so you know what to expect.

What to prepare and when

Get your quote or certificate of coverage before your medical appointment and bring a printed or digital copy to both your medical and your interview. If you are submitting a public charge statement to LNDIVSubmissions@state.gov ahead of your interview, include it there too. You are not required to have purchased a policy. A quote demonstrating you have researched and budgeted for coverage is sufficient. One exception worth considering: if you have an ongoing condition that will come up at your medical, actual confirmed coverage answers the "who pays for that care" question far better than a quote does.

Common Questions

Can the petitioner come to the medical or the interview? The two are different. Not the embassy: petitioners are not permitted inside the US Embassy for either the document check or the interview. The beneficiary attends alone; the only exceptions are an interpreter, or one accompanying person for a visitor with special needs. The medical is more flexible: Visa Medicals will allow a companion to wait, which is worth arranging if you need someone to mind children. Pre-arrange it with the clinic by email, roughly a week ahead. Set expectations, though: the waiting area is small and gets very hot, and the building is not step-free, so a pram needs assistance.

Do I need the original I-864 mailed to me? No. A printed copy is sufficient.

Do I need two separate trips to London? Unless you have an expedited interview, everyone makes two separate trips: one for the medical and document check, one for the interview. There is no exception for distance or cost. Plan accommodation and travel well in advance.

Expedited interviews

Expedited interviews are available in genuine emergency situations such as serious medical need. They are not a workaround for queue position. Community data shows 4 expedited cases. These are excluded from wait time calculations as they are not representative of the standard process.

To request: email NVCexpedite@state.gov
