Stage 5: Medical exam

⏱ 1 day in London: 1–2 hours at the medical, plus the same-day document check at the embassy £400 for adults 45+, £430–£450 for younger adults, plus any tests or vaccines At least 10 working days before your interview Reviewed 27 Sept 2026

Bottom line

You are here: You have your interview date. You need a medical before the interview can happen.

What happens: One clinic covers the whole of the UK: VisaMedicals in London. Your medical must be at least 10 working days before your interview. Appointment windows open once a month and fill fast. Budget £400 if you are 45 or older, or £430–£450 for a younger adult, plus any tests or vaccines.

Do this first: Call VisaMedicals on 020 7486 7822 as soon as your window opens.

Action list

  1. Either: Book VisaMedicals as soon as the monthly window opens, on 020 7486 7822 or bookings@visamedicals.co.uk. Have ready: the full name and date of birth of every applicant, the case (LND) number, visa category, interview date and ACRO police certificate.
  2. Beneficiary: Ask your GP for a Summary Care Record dated 2–3 weeks before the medical, and a printout of your vaccination records.
  3. Beneficiary: Fill in the medical questionnaire (IV18a). Register your passport return on AIS to get the courier confirmation with its QR code.
  4. Beneficiary: If you have a significant medical or mental health history, get reports from the clinician who treats you.
  5. Beneficiary: Keep the pink checklist slip safe after the medical. You need it for the embassy document check, which is canceled for now.

Timing and booking

The medical must be at least 10 working days before your interview. VisaMedicals opens appointments a month at a time: for a May interview, you can book from about 1 April. Book as soon as the window opens, because slots fill fast. After the medical, the same day, you must go to the embassy document check (window: 11:45 AM – 2:30 PM). If you are running late, VisaMedicals tells the embassy for you. If you cancel within 3 working days of your appointment, VisaMedicals charges £100.

When you call to book, have ready: the full name and date of birth of every applicant, the case (LND) number, visa category, interview date, ACRO police certificate, and a phone number or email for you.

Your medical is valid for 6 months, and so is your window to enter the US

Medical results last 6 months from the date of the exam. The embassy issues the visa to match, so you must enter the US on the visa within those same 6 months, counted from the medical, not the interview.

Two things follow. A rescheduled interview or re-interview must still fall inside those 6 months, with time left to book travel. And every week between the medical and the interview is a week less to plan the move. Work out both dates with the interview date checker.

GP Summary Care Record

Ask your GP for a Summary Care Record dated 2–3 weeks before your medical. It should show your details and the GP's, significant medical history, current medicines and ongoing problems. Some practices add recent appointments and vaccinations. The practice does not need to sign or stamp the record or the vaccination history.

If you have a significant medical history, such as cancer, HIV, TB, depression or another serious condition, bring reports that set out the diagnosis, treatment and where things stand now. If the doctor needs more, they tell you on the day, and you can email it afterwards. VisaMedicals deals with many situations. Contact them beforehand with any questions.

What to bring to the medical

  • Passport
  • Interview letter The email from NVC with your interview date and place.
  • DS-260 confirmation page
  • Courier registration confirmation (with QR code) You get this when you register your passport return on the AIS website (ais.usvisa-info.com).
  • Completed medical questionnaire (IV18a)
  • ACRO police certificate
  • GP summary dated within 2–3 weeks The surgery does not need to sign or stamp it. A printed or digital summary from your NHS record is enough.
  • Vaccination records Nobody needs to sign or stamp it. A printout from the NHS app or your GP record is fine.
  • Glasses or contact lenses (if applicable)
  • Medical reports for any chronic, serious, or mental health conditions (if applicable)
  • Psychiatric documentation (if applicable)
  • TB or STI records (if applicable)
  • Child health records (if applicable)

What happens at the medical

The medical checks the health grounds for entry to the US. CDC guidance on the medical history and physical examination →

  • Check-in and digital photo
  • Document review
  • Chest X-ray (age 15+)
  • Blood test (age 18+)
  • Urine test (age 18+)
  • Physical examination (in a gown)
  • Vaccine review and administration

At the end you get a pink checklist slip. Take it to the same-day embassy document check, and do not lose it. While document checks are canceled, keep it safe until they resume.

Vaccinations

Common requirements: MMR, Tetanus/Diphtheria, Hepatitis A & B, Varicella (or documented history), Polio (within 12 months: see CDC Polio addendum for UK-specific requirements), Flu (Oct–Mar only). Missing vaccines can cost less from your GP beforehand than at the clinic. COVID-19 is no longer required. Hepatitis B takes three doses, but you only need to have started: you can finish the rest in the US. Bring official records of past vaccinations. The clinic will often take your word that you had chickenpox as a child.

Full CDC vaccination requirements for immigrants →

Beneficiary: Vaccine waivers

If a vaccine is medically wrong for you (unsafe for you, wrong for your age, not routinely available, or out of flu season), the panel physician records a blanket waiver on the medical form. You do not need to apply. If you refuse a vaccine because of a sincere religious or moral objection to all vaccination, you are inadmissible under INA 212(a)(1)(A)(ii). You then need an individual waiver on Form I-601 under INA 212(g)(2)(C), which USCIS decides. The embassy cannot issue the visa until USCIS approves the waiver, and that can add many months. Check current USCIS processing times rather than relying on a fixed figure.

Beneficiary: Pregnancy considerations

Everyone 15 or older needs a chest X-ray. You can put it off until after the birth or after the 12-week scan. You cannot have live vaccines (MMR, varicella) while pregnant. Putting things off may delay the visa.

Beneficiary: Mental health

The medical includes a mental health assessment. Depression, anxiety and other mental health diagnoses do not by themselves stop you entering the US. The doctor looks at whether a condition comes with harmful behavior. If you have a mental health history, bring records from the clinician who treats you.

CDC guidance on mental health assessment →
Beneficiary: Transgender and non-binary applicants: document consistency

Under US policy since April 2, 2025, USCIS recognizes only male and female on immigration documents. It takes sex from the birth certificate issued at or nearest the time of birth. USCIS has removed the X marker from all its forms and documents.

Your documents may show different sex markers: your UK passport or gender recognition certificate may differ from your birth certificate, or your medical records may differ from both. Expect delays or a Request for Evidence. VisaMedicals records sex as your documents show it, which may add another mismatch to the case file.

Practical steps:

  • Before you submit to NVC or go to the medical, check the sex marker on your birth certificate, passport and any other identity documents.
  • If they all match, use the marker on your original birth certificate on every immigration form, to keep delays down.
  • If they do not match, or your birth certificate does not say male or female, get advice from an immigration attorney experienced in LGBTQ+ cases before you go further. Use the AILA directory to find one.

Support resources:

  • Immigration Equality: free and low-cost immigration legal help.
  • National Center for Transgender Equality: Immigration Documents

Same-day embassy document check

After the medical, go straight to the embassy.

Embassy document check

Address: 33 Nine Elms Lane, London SW11 7US

Window: 11:45 AM – 2:30 PM. Go even if you are running late.

Getting there from VisaMedicals:

  • Victoria line towards Brixton from Oxford Circus → Vauxhall, ~10 min walk: £3.10 contactless
  • Bus 2 towards West Norwood from Portman Sq → Vauxhall Bus Station, ~12 min walk: £1.75 contactless
Running late is not a problem

When you finish at the medical, the clinic tells the embassy you are on your way. If you go straight there, the embassy will see you for the document check. Do not worry about the posted closing time.

Finding the Pass Back window

Do not join the main visa queue. The document check starts at a separate "Pass Back" window on the south side of the embassy, and it is easy to walk past.

1
Head for the south pavilion entrance
Walking from Vauxhall station, it is on your right. The Pass Back window is the first window you reach, just behind the security booth with guards inside.
2
Look for the "PASSBACK" sign
Shortly before the window opens, staff swap a podium for a sign headed "PASSBACK" with a red arrow, reading "Immigrant/Fiancé(e) Visa Document Review, 11:45 AM – 2:30 PM". The sign stands directly opposite the window. Ignore the 1:30–2:30 PM passport collection line on the same sign. If you arrive before about 11:30 and see no queue, you are early, not lost.
3
Check in at the window
Show your pink checklist slip and passport. Staff put a numbered sticky note on your passport and send you in when entry opens, at about 11:45.

Entrance doors on the left, the Pass Back window on the right (with sign). Do not join the main queue. Go directly to Pass Back.

Once you are inside

The embassy is two buildings with an open courtyard between them. The document check is upstairs in the second building. Signs and staff show the way, but it helps to know where you are going.

1
Security in the first building
Airport-style screening in the first building you enter. Have your passport and DS-260 confirmation to hand.
2
Cross the open courtyard
Out of security, walk through the open courtyard to the second building.
3
Check in again at the desk
Staff at a desk in the second building check you in again and send you upstairs.
4
Out of the lifts: left, left at the end of the room, then left again
Turn left out of the lifts. Walk to the end of the room and turn left, then turn left again into the waiting area. The first two lefts alone bring you to a queue, and members have joined it by mistake. Keep going until you reach a small waiting area with three banks of seats facing a wall of booths. That is the document check, and it is the same room you will come back to for your interview, so it is worth registering the route now.
5
At the booth
When staff call your number, they check your documents and take your fingerprints at the same booth, in one step. They then give you a sealed "Do Not Tamper" envelope holding your documents. Bring it, unopened, to your visa interview.

Staff usually check documents one by one, so separate sleeves or sections make it smoother. Many members use one binder for the medical and another for the embassy.

Bring to the document check

  • Passport
  • Pink checklist slip (from medical)
  • DS-260 confirmation page
  • Civil documents (originals)
Realistic timing

Arrive at the medical at 8:40 AM and finish about 10:15 AM. Have a coffee. The document check queue opens at 11:45 AM, and you finish about 12:15 PM. The document check is usually quick, and staff tell you everything is in order, which many members find reassuring.

What not to bring

  • Laptops or large bags. See the embassy visiting guidelines

Community medical experiences

What ten London members reported, March 2025 to June 2026. Every one found the process professional and manageable.

  • Timing: 1 to 2 hours at the clinic, then 15 to 45 minutes at the embassy document check. Arrive about 10 minutes early to fill in the questionnaire on arrival. Some members were seen before their slot.
  • At reception: staff take your passport, GP summary, vaccination record, ACRO certificate and appointment email, and take your photo on site, so you need no 2x2 photos.
  • The exam: chest X-ray (you change into a gown), the doctor's questions and checks, a blood test, and the nurse for vaccines. The order changes with the clinic's schedule. Some applicants give a urine sample, depending on age.
  • Cost: members paid £430 to £495, depending on vaccines.
  • Afterwards: you leave with the pink slip for the embassy. The clinic contacts you within 3 to 5 working days only if the X-ray or blood tests show a problem. No news means all clear.
  • Records the doctor accepted: a former employer's summary letter for latent TB history, a partial Hep B series, and CBT confirmation with medication records in place of a missing GP report on anxiety.
  • Access: the building is not step-free. A pram needs help through two sets of doors with steps.
  • Small tip: use hand cream beforehand, because fingerprints scan more easily at the document check.
