CRBA: if your child is already a US citizen

⏱ About 4 weeks for a simple UK birth CRBA $100, first passport $135 under 16 Apply before age 18, soon after the birth if you can Reviewed 27 Sept 2026

You are here: Your child was born in the UK to a US citizen parent. Your child may already be a US citizen.

What happens: A child who is already a citizen needs a CRBA (Consular Report of Birth Abroad) and a US passport, not an immigrant visa. This runs separately from the I-130 process. Most cases succeed or fail on the evidence of time the US citizen parent spent in the US, which the law calls physical presence.

Do this first: Find the citizenship rule that fits your family (see who qualifies). Then check the US citizen parent can prove the time in the US that rule needs: usually 5 years before the birth, with 2 of those years after age 14.

Action list

  1. US citizen parent: Gather evidence that you spent at least 5 years in the US before your child’s birth, with at least 2 of those years after you turned 14.
  2. Either parent: Fill in the eCRBA on MyTravelGov and the DS-11 passport form. Do not sign the DS-11. Add the SS-5 if you want a Social Security number for your child. Upload your documents and pay the $100 CRBA fee.
  3. Either parent: Email the London CRBA unit to book the appointment. Give your child’s name, date of birth and CRBA case number.
  4. Either parent: Buy a prepaid Royal Mail Special Delivery return envelope. You must bring one.
  5. Other parent: If you cannot attend, sign a DS-3053 consent form in front of a notary no more than 3 months before the appointment.

A child born in the UK to a US citizen parent may be a US citizen from birth. That child does not need an immigrant visa. They need a CRBA (Consular Report of Birth Abroad) to prove their citizenship, and a US passport to travel. The CRBA runs separately from the I-130 process.

CRBA or I-130?

Check CRBA eligibility before you file anything. If your child is a US citizen from birth, an I-130 for an IR2 visa is the wrong route. A CRBA is faster and simpler, and skips the visa process. One question decides it: does the US citizen parent meet the physical presence rule?

If your child is not a US citizen from birth, file an I-130 for an IR2 visa.

  • Apply before your child turns 18. Soon after the birth is best.
  • Apply before your child travels to the US. US citizens must enter the US on a US passport.
  • One appointment covers the CRBA, the first US passport and, if you want one, a Social Security number application.
  • Your child was born in the UK: apply at the US Embassy in London.
  • Your child was born in another country and lives in the UK: apply in London. London sends the case to the US embassy in the country of birth, so it takes longer.
  • Your child is already in the US: you cannot apply for a CRBA. Apply for a first US passport instead, and the passport office decides your child’s citizenship.

The most common London case is married parents, where one parent is a US citizen and the other is not. The US citizen parent must prove they spent at least 5 years in the US before the child’s birth, with at least 2 of those years after age 14 (INA 301(g)).

Other family situations follow other rules:

  • Married, and both parents are US citizens: one parent must have lived in the US at some point before the birth. There is no 5-year count (INA 301(c)).
  • Unmarried, and the mother is the US citizen: she needs 1 year in the US without a break, at any time before the birth (INA 309(c)).
  • Unmarried, and the father is the US citizen: the 5-year rule applies. He also needs clear and convincing evidence that he is the child’s biological father. He must legitimate or acknowledge paternity before the child turns 18, and agree in writing to support the child until 18 (INA 309(a)).

Check which rule fits your family on the State Department’s acquisition page. For most families, the evidence of time in the US decides the case. Weak evidence leads to delay or refusal. [travel.state.gov: Birth Abroad]

The time does not need to be in one block: visits of any length add up. Count only days spent in the US, and leave out every day abroad, including holidays and short trips. It does not matter whether you were in the US legally or illegally, or whether you were a US citizen at the time.

What counts as time in the US
  • Time inside the US. Separate stays add up.
  • Honorable US military service abroad, or time abroad as the dependent of someone serving. You need official military records.
  • Work abroad for the US government or certain international organizations, or time abroad as the dependent of someone doing that work. You need official records.
Evidence that works, and evidence that doesn’t

The embassy accepts:

  • Official school transcripts: primary, secondary or university
  • W-2 forms, with a letter from the employer’s HR department
  • Passports that show both US entries and exits. If you used several passports, the dates can be hard to piece together.
  • Military records: a Statement of Service or DD-214
  • Bank or credit card statements that show spending at a named place in the US, such as cash machine withdrawals or restaurant bills
  • US medical records, which count only for the exact dates of treatment
  • Your CBP entry and exit records, which you request under FOIA at cbp.gov

The embassy does not accept these as proof of time in the US:

  • A US driver’s license: it does not show when you were there or for how long
  • A diploma without transcripts: you may have earned credits abroad
  • A lease or mortgage: many people keep homes in more than one country
  • Phone records
  • Bank statements that do not show a US location
  • Social media posts about being in the US
  • Tax forms without pay stubs or W-2s: you can file US taxes from anywhere

Sources: US Embassy CRBA: Proving Physical Presence (PDF) · US Embassy London, Oct 2025 Notice

1
Apply online and gather your evidence
Fill in the eCRBA on MyTravelGov and the DS-11 passport form. Leave the DS-11 unsigned until the appointment. For a child under 5, you can also fill in the SS-5 for a Social Security number. Upload your documents and pay online where you can: CRBA $100; passport $135 under 16, $165 at 16–17. Gather all your evidence before you book. Missing evidence causes most delays.
2
Book the appointment
Once the eCRBA is complete, email the London CRBA unit with your child’s name, date of birth and CRBA case number.
3
Go to the appointment
Your child must attend, and so must the US citizen parent. The other parent should attend too. If they cannot, they need a DS-3053 consent form, notarized no more than 3 months before. The consular officer checks eligibility, looks at the physical presence evidence, and may ask for more documents. Bring originals and photocopies of everything.
4
Wait for delivery
A simple case for a child born in the UK usually takes about 4 weeks. Complex cases take longer: a birth outside the UK, for example, or a case where it is unclear which citizenship rule applies. The CRBA certificate and first US passport come back in your prepaid Royal Mail Special Delivery envelope, which you must supply. If you applied for a Social Security number, the SSA sends the card separately, usually about 4 months later.
Your child’s documents
  • Long-form UK birth certificate listing both parents
  • A passport photo
  • Your child’s other passport, if they have one. You keep it.
US citizen parent’s documents
  • Proof of US citizenship: a US passport or naturalization certificate
  • Evidence of time in the US (see who qualifies)
Proof of the parents’ relationship
  • Your marriage certificate, if you are married
  • Divorce or death certificates for any earlier marriages
Other parent’s documents
  • Passport or national ID
  • A notarized DS-3053 consent form, if they are not attending
Do not forget
  • The prepaid Royal Mail Special Delivery return envelope. Bring it to the appointment. Without it, the embassy cannot send back the CRBA and passport.
  • The unsigned DS-11. Sign it at the appointment, not before.
  • Your child does not qualify for a CRBA: you have two other routes. Your child may be able to naturalize from abroad on Form N-600K, using a US citizen grandparent’s time in the US (INA 322). Or your child can immigrate on an I-130 (IR2) and become a citizen automatically on arrival, under the Child Citizenship Act (INA 320).
  • More than one child: each child needs a separate appointment. You can arrange them back to back.
  • Lost CRBA: ask the US Department of State for a replacement, not the London embassy.

Before you move on

  • US citizen parent: My evidence covers 5 years in the US, with 2 after age 14, and leaves out all time abroad.
  • Either parent: The eCRBA is complete, the $100 fee is paid, and the DS-11 is still unsigned.
  • Either parent: I have the long-form UK birth certificate listing both parents, and a passport photo.
  • Either parent: The prepaid Royal Mail Special Delivery envelope is ready for the appointment.
  • Either parent: Our child and the US citizen parent will attend. The other parent will attend too, or has a notarized DS-3053.
