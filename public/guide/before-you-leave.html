Stage 8: Before you leave the UK

Once your visa is in your passport and your flight is booked Some tasks have deadlines Reviewed 27 Sept 2026

Bottom line

You are here: Your visa is in your passport and your flight is booked.

What happens: You have UK obligations to deal with before you leave. Some have deadlines that can affect your money or your rights after you go. The costliest one people miss is ISAs: you need to act on them before you become a US person for tax.

Do this first: Speak to a UK/US cross-border tax professional about any ISAs.

Action list

  1. Beneficiary: Before you become a US person, get UK/US cross-border tax advice on any ISAs. Your choices: sell the holdings, move them into US-compliant investments, or accept complex US reporting every year.
  2. Beneficiary: Book travel that gets you to the US within 6 months of your medical exam. Your medical results last 6 months from the exam, and so does your window to enter on the visa. Check your date.
  3. Either: File a P85 with HMRC. It tells HMRC you no longer live in the UK and sets your departure date for UK tax.
  4. Either: If you owe student loans, update your details with the Student Loans Company.
  5. Either: To keep your UK vote, register as an overseas voter before you leave.
  6. Either: Decide whether to keep paying voluntary National Insurance. You need 10 years of contributions for any UK State Pension, and 35 years for the full pension.

Before you leave, you have UK tasks to finish. Some have deadlines that can affect your money or your rights once you have gone. This page lists them with the costliest first.

UK tasks before you leave

Either: ISAs: act before you leave

UK movers to the US miss this more than anything else, and it costs the most. Once you become a US person (a green card holder), your ISAs lose their tax-free status for US tax. Worse, Stocks & Shares ISAs usually hold non-US funds that US tax rules treat as PFICs (Passive Foreign Investment Companies).

The US taxes PFICs heavily. You file Form 8621 for each fund every year, pay punitive tax on gains, lose the lower capital gains rates, and pay high costs to stay compliant.

If you cash out an ISA after you become a US person, PFIC tax applies. Before you become a US person, you can sell the holdings, move them into US-compliant investments, or accept the complex reporting.

Speak to a UK/US cross-border tax professional before you decide anything. Do not assume your UK accountant or a standard US tax preparer will get this right.

Either: File a P85 with HMRC

When you leave the UK, file a P85 form to show you no longer live here. The P85 stops HMRC taxing you wrongly, sets your departure date for UK tax, and may lead to a tax refund. HMRC usually mails refunds as a paper check to your US address, so make sure you have a way to deposit it.

gov.uk: P85 guidance

Either: Update your student loan details

If you still owe student loans, log in to your student loan account and tell the Student Loans Company (SLC) you are moving abroad. The SLC needs this to set your repayments, and you need it so you keep getting messages about your account.

Tell the embassy too. The consular officer weighs a UK student loan as a debt against your income. Put it in your public charge statement: the balance, the expected monthly repayment and which income pays it. Attach your latest statement. See public charge: student loans.

gov.uk: Update student loan details when moving abroad

Either: National Insurance

You can keep paying voluntary National Insurance from the US. Think it through:

  • 10 years of contributions gets you the minimum UK State Pension
  • 35 years gets you the full UK State Pension
  • Voluntary contributions cost relatively little, and the State Pension is index-linked, so it rises over time. Many UK emigrants find them good value.

gov.uk: NI contributions abroad

Either: The NHS after you move

The NHS depends on where you live, not your citizenship. Once you move to the US for good, you lose free access to most NHS care. The exceptions:

  • A&E: always free. Anyone can use A&E in the UK, wherever they live.
  • Everything else: the NHS charges overseas visitors for GP visits, prescriptions, hospital treatment and ongoing care. A consultation or treatment can cost hundreds of pounds.
  • If you move back to the UK: free access returns as soon as you live in the UK again.

Plan ahead: look at health insurance for the US. Expect NHS charges if you visit the UK for treatment while you live abroad. See NHS: Planning your healthcare abroad.

Either: Register as an overseas voter

To keep your UK vote, register before you leave: gov.uk/voting-when-living-abroad

Before you move on

  • Beneficiary: I have had cross-border tax advice on any ISAs, before I become a US person.
  • Either: My P85 is filed, and I have a way to deposit an HMRC paper check at my US address.
  • Either: If I have a student loan, the Student Loans Company knows I am moving abroad.
  • Either: If I want to keep my UK vote, I have registered as an overseas voter.
  • Either: I know the NHS will charge me for most care once I move. Only A&E stays free.
