Step 5: Prepare for the English and civics test

Reviewed 11 Nov 2026

You are here: Your N-400 is filed, or about to be, and you want to be ready for the tests at your interview.

What happens: At the interview the officer asks civics questions aloud and tests your English: speaking, reading one sentence and writing one sentence. Both tests happen at the same appointment as your N-400 review.

Do this first: Check which civics test you take, then start the flashcards.

Action list

  1. Check which civics test you take. If USCIS received your N-400 on or after October 20, 2025, it is the 2025 test: up to 20 of 128 questions, 12 correct to pass.
  2. Work through the civics flashcards until every card is marked known.
  3. In the week before your interview, check the answers that change, such as who holds each office, on the USCIS civics test updates page.
  4. Practise reading and writing the sentences on the English test practice page.
  5. Check whether you qualify for an exemption because of your age and years as a permanent resident.

Documents for this stage

  • English test practice: the reading and writing vocabulary, with practice sentences
  • Civics flashcards (PDF): all 128 questions, to print
  • English flashcards (PDF): the reading and writing vocabulary, to print
Study tool · civics test

Civics test flashcards

All 128 questions and answers of the 2025 civics test, word for word from the USCIS list "128 Civics Questions and Answers (2025 version)" (M-1778, 09/25). It applies to every N-400 filed on or after October 20, 2025. You need 12 correct out of up to 20. The test is spoken: the officer reads each question aloud and you answer aloud. The officer stops once you have enough correct answers to pass, or once you can no longer pass. For each question, give one listed answer, or as many as the question asks for.

Filed before October 20, 2025?

If USCIS received your N-400 before October 20, 2025, you take the older 2008 civics test: up to 10 of 100 questions, 6 correct to pass. These flashcards do not cover it. Study from the 2008 list of 100 questions and the other 2008 materials on the USCIS study page.

Answers that change, and answers that depend on where you live

Some answers depend on who holds office when you interview: the President, Vice President, Speaker of the House, and Chief Justice. Answer with whoever holds the office on your interview day, and check uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates the week before. Four answers depend on where you live: your senators, your representative, your governor and your state capital. Look them up for your address. The cards give current names as a study note, separate from the official answer.

    The English test

    At the interview the officer also tests your English: speaking (through your answers about your N-400), reading one sentence aloud, and writing one sentence. For most applicants from the UK this needs no extra study. If you want to practice, the USCIS reading and writing word lists and the interview vocabulary are on Checklists & Tools.

    Test exemptions

    At the time you file, you are…English testCivics test
    50 or older, and a permanent resident for 20+ years (50/20)WaivedFull test, in your language with an interpreter
    55 or older, and a permanent resident for 15+ years (55/15)WaivedFull test, in your language with an interpreter
    65 or older, and a permanent resident for 20+ years (65/20)WaivedOnly the 20 starred questions: up to 10 asked, 6 correct to pass, in your language
    Unable to learn because of a medical disabilityCan be waived with Form N-648, completed by a licensed medical professional (no fee)

    Source: USCIS: Exceptions and Accommodations; Form N-648. If you use a language exemption, you bring your own interpreter.
