Delays, denials and your options

After you file · Form N-400 Reviewed 11 Nov 2026

You are here: You have filed your N-400 and your case is taking a long time, or USCIS has denied it.

What happens: Naturalization rates have fallen sharply in 2025 and 2026 [Bloomberg Tax]. Plan for a long wait, and know your deadlines if USCIS stalls or denies your case.

Key deadlines: 120 days after your interview with no decision, you can go to federal court. After a denial, you have 30 days to request a hearing.

Check your own field office

Look up your field office on the USCIS processing times tool (choose Form N-400 and your office). The backlog falls unevenly. Some offices have a few months of pending cases; others have years. The published time measures cases USCIS has already finished, so it can trail a growing backlog. USCIS also publishes pending cases by office in its quarterly N-400 data.

Your options

No decision 120 days after your interview
You can file suit in the federal district court where you live under 8 U.S.C. 1447(b). The court can decide your case or send it back to USCIS with instructions. A suit forces a decision, not an approval. If you settle, you may not recover your legal fees: in Avdeeva v. Tucker (1st Cir. 2025) the court held that an applicant who settled was not a "prevailing party" under the Equal Access to Justice Act.
Your case is denied
File Form N-336 within 30 days of the decision to request a hearing. If that fails, you can ask a federal district court to review the denial under 8 U.S.C. 1421(c).
An old form does not match
USCIS reviews your whole immigration history again. In Savane v. Secretary DHS (3d Cir. Jan. 2026) the court upheld a denial because the applicant left two children off his DS-230 immigrant visa form years earlier. The court held the omission material because it shut off a line of questioning, even though nobody could show it would have changed the outcome. Check your N-400 against every earlier form before you file.

For help with a suit or a hearing, see free and low-cost legal help.
