Step 8: After you become a citizen

Reviewed November 11, 2026

You are here: You have taken the Oath of Allegiance and have your Certificate of Naturalization. Congratulations.

What happens: A few records need updating, and your new status changes things for your family. Your children may already be citizens, and petitions you filed for relatives may now move faster.

Do this first: Put your certificate somewhere safe, and apply for a US passport.

Action list

  1. Apply for a US passport, and for your children who became citizens with you.
  2. After at least 10 days, update your Social Security record.
  3. Register to vote.
  4. If you have children under 18 with green cards, check whether they are already citizens.
  5. If you filed petitions for relatives as a green card holder, find out what happens to them now.
  6. Update your driver's license or state ID if your name or address changed.
  7. Tell your employer, bank and any benefit programs, and use your new status on your next tax return. See other records to update.

Official resources

  • USCIS: New US citizens: the official checklist this page is based on
  • The Citizen's Almanac (M-76): the rights and responsibilities of citizenship

Apply for a US passport

A US passport is proof of citizenship, like your certificate, and much easier to carry. Apply through the Department of State. Your Citizenship Welcome Packet includes an application form.

  • Apply in person. A first passport uses Form DS-11, filed in person at a passport acceptance facility. Many post offices are acceptance facilities. You also need a passport photo.
  • Send your original Certificate of Naturalization and a photocopy with your application. The original is returned to you.
  • Leave plenty of time before any trip abroad. As a US citizen, you must use a US passport to leave and enter the US.
  • Passport book or card: the book works for all international travel. The cheaper card works only for land and sea travel to Canada, Mexico, the Caribbean and Bermuda. You can have both.
  • Your children: you can also apply for a passport for any child under 18 who became a citizen when you naturalized. See your children.

Keeping your other citizenship

The oath includes renouncing other allegiances, but the US does not take away your other citizenship or ask you to prove you gave it up. Whether you keep it depends on your other country's law. The UK allows dual citizenship, so British citizens keep their British citizenship and passport. If you are a citizen of another country, check its rules before your ceremony. When you travel, use your US passport to leave and enter the US.

[USCIS: New US citizens]

Update your Social Security record

Your Social Security record still shows you as a noncitizen until you update it. Employers use it through E-Verify to check that you can work, so an old record can cause problems with a new job.

  • You may already be updated. Recent editions of the N-400 let you ask USCIS to share your information with Social Security. If you said yes, check your record in your my Social Security account before doing anything else.
  • If it is not updated, wait at least 10 days after your ceremony.
  • Then fill in Form SS-5 and submit it by mail or at a Social Security office, with your certificate or US passport.
  • Questions: call 800-772-1213 or visit ssa.gov.

Register to vote

Voting is a right and a responsibility of citizenship. You must register before you can vote. Go to vote.gov to find how to register in your state: online, by mail, in person, or when you apply for or renew your driver's license. You may also get a registration form at your ceremony.

Never register or vote before you take the oath. Voting as a noncitizen can end a naturalization case and lead to removal. The Voter's Guide to Federal Elections explains how elections work.

Your children may already be citizens

A child becomes a US citizen automatically on the day you naturalize if, on that day, the child:

  • is under 18,
  • has a green card, and
  • lives in the US in your legal and physical custody.

They do not need to file an N-400 or take an oath. To get proof of their citizenship, apply for a US passport for them, or file Form N-600 for a Certificate of Citizenship. Stepchildren do not qualify unless you have adopted them. See children in the US for the full rules. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part H, Ch. 4]

Petitions you filed for relatives

If you filed a Form I-130 for a relative while you had a green card, your citizenship can change their category. They keep their original priority date. [Policy Manual Vol. 6, Part B, Ch. 2]

Your spouse or child under 21 (F2A)

The petition converts automatically to an immediate relative petition (IR1 for a spouse, IR2 for a child). Immediate relatives do not wait for a visa number, so this usually speeds things up a lot.

Children listed on a spouse's petition

Immediate relatives cannot bring "derivative" family members. If your spouse's F2A petition listed their children as derivatives, those children lose that place when the petition converts. File a separate I-130 for each child, as their parent or stepparent. A stepchild qualifies only if you married their parent before the child turned 18.

Your unmarried son or daughter, 21 or older (F2B)

The petition converts automatically to first preference (F1), unmarried son or daughter of a US citizen. Sometimes F2B moves faster than F1 for a given country. If so, your son or daughter can opt out of the conversion and stay in F2B. They ask in writing to the USCIS office that approved the petition, or through the USCIS Contact Center. Check the Visa Bulletin for both categories first.

Tell the agency handling the case

Send proof of your citizenship, such as a copy of your certificate or US passport, with your case or receipt number, to whoever has the case now: USCIS if the petition is still pending, or the National Visa Center (NVC) if it is approved. If your relative is already scheduled for a visa interview abroad, tell the embassy as well.

New relatives you can now sponsor

As a citizen, you can petition for relatives a green card holder cannot:

  • Your parents, if you are 21 or older (immediate relatives, no visa wait).
  • Your married sons and daughters (third preference, F3).
  • Your brothers and sisters, if you are 21 or older (fourth preference, F4).

You must also show you can support them financially, usually with Form I-864. See USCIS: Family of US citizens, and our family visa guide for the whole process. [Policy Manual Vol. 6, Part B, Ch. 2]

Driver's license and state ID

If your name or address changed, update your record with your state's driver's license agency, often called the DMV. Find yours on USA.gov. Some states also issue licenses that end when your green card expires, so check whether yours needs updating to show your new status.

Other records to update

  • Your employer: tell human resources and show your certificate or passport. Some jobs, including many federal jobs and roles that need a security clearance, are open only to citizens.
  • Banks and lenders: update your citizenship status with your bank, credit union and any lenders.
  • Taxes: there is no form to tell the IRS. Use your current legal name and status on your next tax return.
  • Benefit programs: if you get state benefits such as Medicaid, SNAP or housing assistance, tell the program about your new status. Veterans should give the VA a copy of their certificate.
  • Selective Service: men aged 18 to 25 who live in the US must be registered, whether or not they are citizens. If you registered as a green card holder, you do not need to register again. Check your status at sss.gov.
  • Your will or estate plan: ask an attorney to review it. Estate tax rules can work differently for US citizens, especially if your spouse is not a citizen.

Keeping your certificate safe

  • Keep the original flat and unlaminated, somewhere secure such as a fireproof safe or a bank safe deposit box. See your Certificate of Naturalization.
  • Write down the certificate number and date and keep it separately. It will help if you ever need a replacement.
  • If it is lost or stolen, file Form N-565 online for a replacement. For a stolen certificate, include evidence such as a police report. Until the replacement arrives, your US passport is proof of citizenship.
