When can you apply?

Tool · Step 1 of Become a US citizen Reviewed 27 Sept 2026

You are here: You have a green card through a family visa and want to know the earliest date you can file Form N-400.

What happens: Answer the questions below. The result shows your earliest filing date, then the forms, fees and documents that apply to you.

Do this first: Have your green card to hand. You need its "Resident since" date.

Calculator

When can you file, and what will you need?

  1. Green card
  2. File Form N-400
  3. Biometrics
  4. Interview, English and civics tests
  5. Decision
  6. Oath ceremony

Choose the family visa category you immigrated on and enter your dates. The calculator follows the 3-year and 5-year rules in the USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 12, including the 90-day early filing window. It then lists the forms, fees and documents for your situation. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing you enter is sent or stored.

  • Married to and living with a US citizen (IR1, CR1, K-1): 3 years as a permanent resident, with at least 18 months physically in the US. CR1 holders must also have filed the I-751.
  • Every other family category: 5 years as a permanent resident, with at least 30 months physically in the US.
  • You can file up to 90 days early. An absence of more than 6 months suggests you broke continuous residence. An absence of a year or more breaks it.
Your visa category is printed on your immigrant visa stamp. On the green card it appears as a class code, such as CR1 or IR1 (for K-1 adjustment, CF1 or IR6).
Printed on your green card. For a CR1 or CR2, use the date on your first, conditional card.
Used for the age-18 rule, English test exemptions, and Selective Service.
The 3-year rule needs both, for the whole 3 years before you file.
If they were born a citizen, enter their date of birth.

This calculator estimates dates. It cannot test continuous residence, physical presence or good moral character, which the officer decides from your full record. Before you file, confirm your date with the USCIS early filing calculator and the Policy Manual, Vol. 12, Part D, Ch. 6.
