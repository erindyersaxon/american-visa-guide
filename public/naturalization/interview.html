Step 6: Your citizenship interview

Reviewed November 11, 2026

You are here: You have had your biometrics appointment, and you have an interview notice, or you are waiting for one.

What happens: You meet a USCIS officer in person at your local field office. Under oath, the officer goes through your N-400 with you, checks your documents, and gives you the English and civics tests. You leave with a written result on Form N-652.

Do this first: Reread the copy of the N-400 you filed, and start gathering the originals to bring.

Action list

  1. Check the date, time and field office address on your interview notice. If you cannot go, ask to reschedule right away.
  2. Reread your N-400 and note anything that has changed since you filed: a new address, job, trip, marriage, child, arrest or citation.
  3. Gather every original document you sent copies of, plus your passports and tax transcripts.
  4. Practice the English and civics tests.
  5. Arrive early, answer truthfully, and get your Form N-652 before you leave.

Tools for this step

  • Civics flashcards: all 128 questions of the 2025 civics test
  • N-400 preparation list: the interview day checklist
  • USCIS processing times: how long your field office is taking

Where and when: it depends on your field office

Your interview takes place at the USCIS field office that covers your home address. It is a different place from the center where you gave biometrics. Your interview notice gives the address, date and time.

  • How long you wait for an interview depends mostly on your field office. Some offices take a few months, others more than a year. Check your office on the USCIS processing times page (choose Form N-400).
  • If you moved after filing, USCIS may transfer your case to the office covering your new address. If an officer learns at your interview that you moved, they may finish the interview and then send your file to the new office, which will contact you. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part B, Ch. 3]
  • Some field offices hold the oath ceremony on the same day as an approved interview. Others schedule it weeks later, or send you to a court ceremony. See step 7: oath ceremony.

Before your interview, USCIS may also carry out a neighborhood investigation. Character reference letters sent with your N-400 help USCIS decide whether to skip it.

If you cannot go

Ask to reschedule before the interview, following the instructions on your notice. Have a good reason, such as illness or an emergency, and keep proof of your request.

Missing your interview

If you miss your first interview without good cause and do not tell USCIS why within 30 days, USCIS can close your case. You then have 1 year to ask in writing to reopen it, at no fee. If you reopen it, the date of your request becomes your new filing date, which can affect your eligibility. After 1 year, the application is treated as abandoned. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part B, Ch. 4]

What to bring

The officer will check your documents against your answers, so bring originals of everything you sent copies of. [PA-2026-10]

Everyone

  • Your interview notice.
  • Your green card and a state ID or driver's license.
  • Every passport and travel document you have used since you became a permanent resident, including expired ones.
  • Originals of every document you sent with your N-400.
  • IRS tax return transcripts for each year of your 3- or 5-year period, and account transcripts and proof of payment if you owed taxes or had a payment plan.
  • Copies of your character reference letters.
  • A copy of the N-400 you filed.

If they apply to you

  • 3-year rule or a pending I-751: your marriage certificate, proof your spouse is a citizen, and recent evidence that you live together, such as joint tax returns, bank statements, a lease or mortgage, and children's birth certificates. If USCIS is deciding your I-751 at the same interview, your notice may ask your spouse to come too.
  • Any arrest, citation or charge: official court records showing how each one ended, and proof you finished any sentence, probation or fine.
  • Child support: proof you are paying it.
  • Men who lived in the US between ages 18 and 26: proof of Selective Service registration, or a status information letter if you did not register.
  • A name change: the document that changed it.

Evidence of good moral character

Officers now look for positive things in your life, not only an absence of problems. Bring evidence of what applies to you, such as a letter from your employer, school or college records, proof of volunteer work or community involvement, and proof that you care for family members. [PM-602-0188] See step 2.

Who can come with you

  • An attorney or accredited representative. They must file Form G-28 with USCIS. They can advise you, but cannot answer the officer's questions for you. If your representative cannot come, you can sign a waiver and go ahead alone, or ask the officer to reschedule.
  • An interpreter, only if you are exempt from the English test. You bring and pay for the interpreter. They must translate word for word, sign an interpreter's oath, and show government ID. Choose someone with no stake in your case.
  • A family member or helper, if you have a disability and the officer agrees. Ask for this and any other accommodation before your interview.

Otherwise, expect to go into the interview alone. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part B, Ch. 3] [Part C, Ch. 3]

What happens in the interview

  1. Security and check-in. Arrive early. You will go through security and wait to be called.
  2. The oath to tell the truth. The officer places you under oath before asking anything. Everything you say is sworn testimony.
  3. Identity check. The officer checks your ID and green card.
  4. Your N-400, line by line. The officer asks about your answers and checks them against your documents and the results of your background checks. If something has changed or was wrong, tell the officer. They will correct the form, and you sign the changes.
  5. The English test. Your speaking is judged throughout the interview. You also read one sentence out of up to three, and write one sentence out of up to three.
  6. The civics test. The officer asks up to 20 questions from the list of 128. You need 12 correct answers. See step 5.
  7. Your result. The officer gives you Form N-652.

The tests can come at any point in the interview. The officer may also take a written sworn statement about any issue, or record the interview. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part B, Ch. 3]

What the officer asks about

Questions focus on your N-400, your documents, and anything that came up in your background checks or a neighborhood investigation. They can cover:

  • Your personal details, marriages, and any military service
  • How and when you became a permanent resident
  • Your trips outside the US
  • Where you have lived and worked
  • Your moral character and any criminal history, including taxes
  • Your loyalty to the US and attachment to the Constitution
  • Your membership in certain organizations
  • Whether you are willing to take the Oath of Allegiance

How to answer

  • Listen to the whole question, and answer only what is asked.
  • If you do not understand, say so and ask the officer to repeat or rephrase. That is not a failure of the English test.
  • If you do not know or do not remember, say that. Do not guess dates or numbers.
  • Tell the truth, even when the answer seems bad for you. A false answer under oath can be a reason to deny you, separate from the original problem.

Your result: Form N-652

The officer must give you a written notice of results, Form N-652, at the end of every interview, whatever the outcome. Do not leave without it. USCIS has 120 days from your interview to make a decision. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part B, Ch. 4]

Granted

You passed. A second officer reviews every approval before the oath. If USCIS finds new disqualifying information before then, it can reopen your case.

What to do: wait for your oath ceremony notice, or take the oath the same day if your office offers it. Keep your record clean until then.

Continued: you failed part of a test

You get a second chance at the part you failed, 60 to 90 days after your interview.

What to do: keep practicing, and go to the retest. If you miss it without a good reason, USCIS will deny your application.

Continued: the officer needs more evidence (Form N-14)

The officer needs documents before deciding.

What to do: send everything asked for, in one response, by the deadline, usually 30 days. If you do not, USCIS decides on what it already has.

Denied

USCIS found you do not meet a requirement. You will get a written denial explaining why.

What to do: you have 30 days to request a hearing on Form N-336. See your options after a denial.

If USCIS has not decided your case 120 days after your interview, you can ask a federal court to decide it. See no decision after 120 days.
