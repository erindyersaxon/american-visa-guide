Step 3: Fill in and file Form N-400

Reviewed November 11, 2026

You are here: You have checked that you can apply, and you are ready to fill in your application for citizenship.

What happens: You fill in Form N-400 online or on paper, pay the fee, and send copies of your documents. We also recommend sending at least two character reference letters. A few weeks later, USCIS sends a receipt notice and then a biometrics appointment.

Do this first: Work through the N-400 preparation list, and ask two people for character reference letters now. Letters can take weeks to collect.

Action list

  1. Confirm your earliest filing date with the filing date calculator.
  2. Decide whether to file online or on paper. If you want the reduced fee or a fee waiver, you must file on paper.
  3. Ask at least two US citizens who know you well, and who are not relatives, to write character reference letters.
  4. Gather the documents to send, including your IRS tax transcripts.
  5. Fill in every question. Write "None" or "N/A" when nothing applies, and check that your answers match your tax, travel and court records.
  6. Pay the fee, or attach Form I-912 if you are asking for a fee waiver, and submit.
  7. Save a copy of everything you sent, and keep your receipt notice (I-797C) with your green card.

Tools for this step

  • N-400 preparation list: everything to gather before you file, saved in your browser
  • Character letter template: a fill-in letter you can send to the people writing for you
  • Filing date calculator: your earliest filing date, with the forms and fees for your path

Online or on paper?

Most people can choose either. Filing online costs less and lets you upload documents and read your notices in your USCIS account. You must file on paper if you want the reduced fee or a fee waiver.

What to send with your N-400

Send copies, not originals, unless USCIS asks for an original. Bring the originals to your interview. USCIS can deny an application with missing evidence without asking for it first, so check this list before you submit. [PA-2026-05]

Everyone

  • A copy of the front and back of your green card.
  • The fee, or Form I-912 if you are asking for a fee waiver (paper only). Do not send both: USCIS will take the payment and not consider the waiver.
  • At least two character reference letters (recommended, not required).
  • IRS tax return transcripts for each year of your 3- or 5-year period (recommended). If you had a payment plan or paid late, add your account transcripts and proof you have paid in full. See step 2.

If you are using the 3-year rule

  • Proof your spouse has been a US citizen for the last 3 years: a US birth certificate, naturalization or citizenship certificate, Consular Report of Birth Abroad, or the photo page of their US passport.
  • Your current marriage certificate.
  • Proof that every earlier marriage, for you and your spouse, ended: divorce decrees, annulments or death certificates.

If you have a 2-year (conditional) green card

  • A copy of your I-751 receipt notice if it is still pending, or your I-751 approval notice. See conditional residents.

If any of these apply

  • Any arrest, citation or charge, anywhere in the world: official court records showing how each one ended, even if it was dismissed, sealed or expunged. Talk to an accredited representative or attorney before you file.
  • A name change: the marriage certificate, divorce decree or court order that changed it.
  • Military service: Form N-426 (Request for Certification of Military or Naval Service).
  • A disability that keeps you from learning English or civics: Form N-648, filled in by a licensed medical professional. See test exemptions.

USCIS publishes a full list in its Document Checklist (M-477). Name each upload or label each copy for what it is, for example "01 Green card" and "02 Tax transcripts", so the officer can follow it.

Answering the questions

  • Be complete. List every address, job, trip abroad, marriage and child the form asks for, with dates and no gaps.
  • Be consistent. The officer will compare your answers to your tax transcripts, travel records, court records and earlier immigration forms.
  • Be truthful. Answer "yes" if a question applies to you, even if you think it does not matter or the record was sealed. Lying on the N-400 is worse than most things you might be tempted to hide. It can lead to a denial and a finding that you lack good moral character.
  • Do not guess at legal questions. If you are unsure how to answer a "have you ever" question, ask an accredited representative or attorney before you file.

The officer will go through your N-400 with you line by line at your interview, so keep a copy and reread it before then. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part B, Ch. 3]

Character reference letters

Our recommendation

Send at least two character reference letters with your N-400. They are optional, but they can help USCIS decide not to investigate you in your neighborhood and at work.

Why letters matter now

Since August 2025, USCIS can carry out neighborhood investigations. An investigator may visit the places where you live and work, covering at least the last 5 years, and talk to neighbors, landlords, coworkers, employers and business contacts about your character and community ties. This usually happens before your interview. [PA-2026-10]

USCIS can skip the investigation, and it uses character letters to help decide. Its policy says sending at least two letters with your N-400 helps it decide whether to skip it, and that not sending letters may count toward a decision to investigate. Not sending letters cannot be the only reason USCIS finds that you lack good moral character. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part B, Ch. 2]

Letters also fit how officers now judge good moral character. They look for positive things in your life, such as community involvement, steady work and family responsibilities, not only an absence of problems. [PM-602-0188]

Who to ask

  • US citizens, if possible. USCIS prefers letters from citizens.
  • People who know you personally, such as neighbors, your employer or manager, coworkers, business partners or clients, a teacher, a coach, or someone you volunteer with.
  • Not your relatives. USCIS does not consider letters from immediate family or other relatives, including in-laws. Your spouse's letter will not count.
  • Choose people who have known you for a long time, ideally several years, and who can give real examples.

What each letter must include

USCIS policy lists four things. A letter missing any of them is weaker.

  1. The writer's details: full name, address, phone number and email, a statement of their US citizenship or immigration status, and a copy of proof, such as their passport photo page or naturalization certificate.
  2. The relationship: how the writer knows you, and for how long.
  3. Specific examples of your honesty, integrity and involvement in the community.
  4. A clear statement about your moral character and your attitude toward the United States and the Constitution.

We also suggest the writer signs and dates the letter. Tell your writers in advance that they will need to share a copy of their proof of citizenship, so they can decide whether they are comfortable with that. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part B, Ch. 2]

Letter template

Send this to your writers as a starting point. Ask them to replace everything in [brackets] and to write the examples in their own words. Letters that all read the same carry less weight.

[Date]

U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services
Re: Character reference for [your full name], date of birth [your date of birth]

To the officer reviewing this application:

My name is [writer's full name]. I live at [writer's full address], and you can reach me at [phone number] or [email]. I am a US citizen. A copy of my [US passport photo page / naturalization certificate / other proof] is attached.

I have known [your name] for [number] years, since [year], as [their neighbor / coworker / manager / fellow volunteer at organization]. I see [him / her / them] [how often and in what setting].

[Two or three specific examples: something they saw you do that shows honesty, reliability, or care for others or the community. For example, how you treat coworkers, help neighbors, or volunteer.]

In my experience, [your name] is a person of good moral character. [He / She / They] respects the laws of the United States and the principles of the Constitution, and I believe [he / she / they] will be a responsible citizen.

I declare that the information in this letter is true to the best of my knowledge.

[Signature]
[Writer's printed full name]

How to send them

  • Online: upload each letter, with the writer's proof of citizenship, in the evidence section of your N-400.
  • Paper: put the letters and the copies of proof behind your other documents, labeled "Character reference letters".
  • Keep copies, and bring them to your interview.

Do not

Write the letter yourself for someone to sign, pay anyone for a letter, or ask for letters from relatives. An officer may contact a writer, so every letter must be true and in the writer's own words.

After you file

  • Receipt notice (I-797C): arrives within a few weeks. It extends your green card for 24 months, so keep the two together. Use the receipt number to check your case status.
  • Biometrics appointment: usually a few weeks after your receipt notice. If you miss it without rescheduling, USCIS treats your application as abandoned.
  • Moving: report any new address within 10 days in your USCIS account or on Form AR-11. Your case may move to a different field office.
  • Keep your record clean: good moral character must continue until you take the oath. Keep filing and paying taxes on time, and avoid long trips abroad.
