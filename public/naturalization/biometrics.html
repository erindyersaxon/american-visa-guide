Step 4: Biometrics appointment

Reviewed November 11, 2026

You are here: You have filed your N-400 and have your receipt notice. Next comes a short appointment to take your fingerprints, photo and signature.

What happens: USCIS mails you an appointment notice for an Application Support Center (ASC) near where you live. You go at the date and time on the notice. USCIS uses your fingerprints for FBI and other background checks.

Do this first: Check that USCIS has your current address, and watch your mail and your USCIS account for the appointment notice.

Action list

  1. Watch for your appointment notice (Form I-797C) in the mail and in your USCIS account.
  2. Look up the Application Support Center on the notice and plan how you will get there. It is often not the same building as your USCIS field office.
  3. If you cannot go, reschedule before the appointment date.
  4. On the day, bring your notice, green card and photo ID.
  5. Keep the stamped notice. It is your proof that you attended.

Tools for this step

  • USCIS office locator: find your Application Support Center and field office by ZIP code
  • N-400 preparation list: what to do while your case is pending

What biometrics are, and why you need a new appointment

"Biometrics" means your fingerprints, a photo and your signature. USCIS must collect them from every naturalization applicant, whatever their age. It sends your fingerprints to the FBI for a criminal history check, and uses your photo on your Certificate of Naturalization. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part B, Ch. 2]

You must go even if you gave biometrics for your green card. USCIS can reuse old photos for some applications, but not for the N-400. It requires new biometrics, including a new photo. [PA-2025-29]

There is no separate fee. Biometrics are included in the N-400 fee. [USCIS Fee Schedule, G-1055]

Where you go depends on where you live

USCIS chooses the Application Support Center from the address on your N-400. There are ASCs across the US, but not in every city, so some people travel an hour or more.

  • Your ASC and your field office are different places. The ASC only takes biometrics. Your interview later happens at your USCIS field office. Use the USCIS office locator to find both.
  • The notice goes to the address USCIS has. If you moved after filing, report your new address within 10 days in your USCIS account or on Form AR-11, or you may miss the notice.
  • Walk-ins are not guaranteed. Some ASCs let you come before your appointment date. This is up to each center, so take your notice and be ready to be turned away.

When it happens

The appointment notice usually arrives a few weeks after your receipt notice, and the appointment is usually a few weeks after that. It varies by ASC. The date, time and address are on the notice.

If you filed online, the notice also appears in your USCIS online account.

What to bring

  • Your appointment notice (Form I-797C).
  • Your green card.
  • A second photo ID, such as a driver's license, state ID or passport.

You do not need your documents or N-400 copy for this appointment. Save those for your interview.

On the day

  1. Arrive a little before your appointment time. You will go through a security check.
  2. Staff check your notice and ID.
  3. They scan your fingerprints, take your photo, and ask you to sign on a pad.
  4. They stamp your appointment notice. Keep it.

Most appointments are short. ASC staff take biometrics only. They cannot answer questions about your case.

If your fingerprints cannot be read, for example because of age, skin conditions or work that wears down your fingertips, USCIS may ask you to come back, or ask for police clearance letters from places you have lived. Respond by the deadline on the letter.

If you cannot go: rescheduling and missed appointments

Do not just skip it

If you miss your biometrics appointment without good cause and without telling USCIS, USCIS treats your N-400 as abandoned and denies it. You would lose your fee and have to file again. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part B, Ch. 2]

  • Reschedule before the appointment, in your USCIS online account or by calling the USCIS Contact Center. You need a good reason, such as illness, a medical appointment, a family emergency or travel you cannot change.
  • Keep proof that you asked to reschedule, such as a screenshot or the date and time of your call.
  • If you missed it because of an emergency, contact USCIS as soon as you can and explain why.
  • Avoid long trips abroad while your case is pending. It is easier to keep your appointment, and your continuous residence must continue until the oath.

If you need an accommodation because of a disability, such as wheelchair access or help with fingerprinting, ask through the USCIS Contact Center before your appointment. [Policy Manual Vol. 12, Part C, Ch. 2]

After your appointment

  • Background checks: USCIS runs your fingerprints and an FBI name check. Most people hear nothing about this step.
  • Neighborhood investigation: USCIS may now interview your neighbors, landlord or coworkers, usually before your interview. Sending character reference letters with your N-400 helps it decide whether to skip this. If an investigation turns up something negative, USCIS must tell you and give you a chance to respond. [PA-2026-10]
  • Interview notice: the next notice you get should be for your interview at your field office. How long that takes depends on your field office. See delays and denials if it is taking much longer than the published processing time.
  • Study: use the wait to prepare for the English and civics test.
