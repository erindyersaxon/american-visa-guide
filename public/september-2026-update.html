The immigrant visa pause has been vacated

Posted 12 September 2026. Reviewed 4 November 2026. We update this page as the case moves.

In short

  • A court struck down the 75-country immigrant visa pause. On 10 September 2026 the State Department told every post to stop applying it.
  • Refusals made only because of the pause go back to an officer to decide again. You do not refile or pay again.
  • The Department has set no finish date. London is calling pause cases back for a full interview.
  • The 19 October 2026 Second Circuit stay paused the order on individual refusals. The policy itself stays vacated. Details.

What to do now

If the pause caught your case:

  • Do not start again. The court set your refusal aside. Nothing needs refiling.
  • Renew stale documents now. Medicals and police certificates expire. Three named plaintiffs left their October interviews with a new 221(g) because their medical had run out.
  • Check email and your AIS account daily. London gave one applicant less than 24 hours' notice of a new date.
  • Prepare for public charge questions. The officer fills in a worksheet covering all five statutory factors. See public charge.
  • Got a new 221(g)? Officers must state the specific reason. See 221(g) and re-interview after a 221(g).

Where the case stands

The judgment set aside the policy and each refusal made only under it. On 19 October 2026 the Second Circuit temporarily stayed the second part while it decides the government's stay motion. No post may apply the pause.

On 23 October the Department told the district court its review instructions have not changed. It asked the court not to set deadlines during the stay. The court has not ruled.

The Department counts about 43,000 pause refusals and gives no finish date. Between 21 August and 30 September officers decided about 706 again: roughly 318 approved, 388 refused.

London is calling pause cases back for a full interview. Bring your passport and all your documents. The officer looks at anything that changed since the refusal, such as the petitioner moving abroad.

The pause and the court ruling

From 21 January 2026, officers refused under 221(g) every immigrant visa applicant from 75 countries the Department rated high risk for public charge. Nationality triggered it, not the embassy. A non-UK passport holder interviewing in London could be caught.

On 21 August 2026 the Southern District of New York vacated the pause in CLINIC v. Rubio. Vacatur removes the policy for everyone, not only the plaintiffs. Full write-up: the nationality visa pause, struck down.

A separate pause on diversity visas was also struck down. It does not affect family immigrant visas.

What posts have been told

Cable 26 STATE 88862 (10 September 2026) rescinds the pause in full. No one may be refused under 221(g) because of it. Posts must:

  • find every pause refusal and work them oldest first;
  • write to applicants for anything missing, including new medicals;
  • give the specific reason for any fresh 221(g);
  • complete the public charge worksheet in every immigrant visa case.

The cable expects some applicants to attend a second interview. A call-back does not signal a problem.

Who goes first

Cable 26 STATE 89798 (14 September) and a Visa Office message of 21 September set the queue. Posts take named plaintiffs and Chief of Mission exception cases first, then everyone else by refusal date. A manager reviews a share of decisions each day, and approved cases go through a further review (IVAR) before the visa prints.

If a post replies that your case is “being reviewed in the order in which they were refused,” it is using the Department's set wording.

UnconfirmedApplicants report that families of US service members and government staff go first. No filed document says so. Unless a Chief of Mission exception request went in for your case, do not count on it.

Why pause cases move and new interviews do not

Cable 26 STATE 86164 (1 September) requires each consular section to finish in-person public charge training before it processes immigrant visas. On 5 October the Department told the CLINIC court that pause cases are exempt from the in-person part.

London is calling pause cases back and has emailed new dates for canceled interviews from 6 November. It has sent no new interview letters since 14 July 2026. No public document says when London finishes its training. See our live notice on cancellations.

What this does not mean

  • You do not have a visa yet. An officer decides your case on its merits. A refusal with a separate, genuine ground stands.
  • Public charge still applies. Officers now use a worksheet and new training, and managers review decisions.
  • The travel ban still applies. Proclamation 10998 suspends immigrant visas for nationals of 39 countries, immediate relatives included. Clearing the pause refusal does not override it.
  • The ruling is not final. The government has appealed it. Check the latest position before you make plans you cannot undo.
