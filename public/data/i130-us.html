I-130s across the US

From USCIS quarterly reports. The latest covers April to June 2026.

The national picture

USCIS figures for every family-based I-130, not only London. Reviewed 20 October 2026.

Family-based immigration contracted sharply in 2026. USCIS approved fewer immediate-relative I-130s from April to June 2026 than in any of the 11 quarters below, and denied a larger share. US embassies and consulates issued 46% fewer family-based immigrant visas in February 2026 than in February 2025, most of the fall among nationals of the 75 countries under the January 2026 pause. A federal court vacated the pause on 21 August 2026; see the nationality visa pause, struck down.

We take no position on immigration policy. We publish the agencies’ own figures so you can plan with them. Expect longer timelines than any official estimate, keep your civil documents and financial evidence current, and check the date on any guidance you rely on, ours included.

USCIS: I-130 petitions for immediate relatives

Spouses, unmarried children under 21 and parents of US citizens (CR1/IR1, CR2/IR2, IR5). These have no annual cap, so USCIS decisions set their pace.

USCIS: I-130 petitions for family preference categories (F1 to F4)

Preference cases have annual caps. Their denial rate has run between 28% and 57% every quarter since October 2023. A high rate here is not new, and does not by itself show a change.

How we calculated these figures
  • Denial rate is denials divided by approvals plus denials in the same quarter. USCIS does not publish a rate; we calculate it.
  • USCIS counts every I-130, including petitions for relatives adjusting status inside the US. The tables do not separate consular cases.
  • We leave out USCIS pending counts. USCIS states that administrative closures and transfers are missing from them, so totals cannot be compared across reports.

Sources: USCIS, Number of Form I-130, Petition for Alien Relative, by Category, Case Status, and USCIS Field Office or Service Center Location, FY2024 Q1 to FY2026 Q3.
