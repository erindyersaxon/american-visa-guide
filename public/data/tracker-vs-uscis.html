Our members wait about as long as everyone else for I-130 approval

From USCIS quarterly reports and timelines members shared, as of 25 November 2026.

London tracker members waited about as long for I-130 approval as everyone else. From January 2025 to March 2026, our members' typical wait stayed close to the figure USCIS publishes for all spouses, parents and children of U.S. citizens. So the tracker's I-130 dates are a fair guide for other London families.

"Typical wait" is the middle wait: half of cases took less time, half took more. It's counted from the date USCIS received the I-130 to the date it was approved. The USCIS figure covers every immediate-relative petition it decided that quarter. Quarters with fewer than 10 members are left out.

How much of London the tracker sees

The State Department publishes how many spouse visas (IR1 and CR1) London issues each month. Next to those counts, the tracker's reported approvals are small: never more than three a month through February 2026. Most members' approvals came after that, in months the State Department hasn't published yet.

How to read this

  • Both medians count petitions decided in the quarter, so they measure the same thing. USCIS's covers spouses, parents and children of U.S. citizens; the tracker's covers members whose case goes through London.
  • Tracker members chose to share their timelines. The close match suggests they're typical at the I-130 stage, but it doesn't prove they're typical later, at NVC or the embassy.
  • Waits are measured from the I-130 priority date to the approval date, in months of 30.4 days. A handful of very short waits are likely upgrades or typing mistakes; they're shown but barely move the median.
  • The coverage figures will change once the State Department publishes March 2026 onwards, which is when most tracker approvals happened.
