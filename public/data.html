The National Picture: Official I-130 and Visa Data

USCIS and State Department figures for every family-based case, not only London. Reviewed 20 October 2026.

Family-based immigration contracted sharply in 2026. USCIS approved fewer immediate-relative I-130s from April to June 2026 than in any of the 11 quarters below, and denied a larger share. US embassies and consulates issued 46% fewer family-based immigrant visas in February 2026 than in February 2025, most of the fall among nationals of the 75 countries under the January 2026 pause. A federal court vacated the pause on 21 August 2026; see the nationality visa pause, struck down.

We take no position on immigration policy. We publish the agencies’ own figures so you can plan with them. Expect longer timelines than any official estimate, keep your civil documents and financial evidence current, and check the date on any guidance you rely on, ours included.

USCIS: I-130 petitions for immediate relatives

Spouses, unmarried children under 21 and parents of US citizens (CR1/IR1, CR2/IR2, IR5). These have no annual cap, so USCIS decisions set their pace.

State Department: family-based immigrant visas issued

Visas issued worldwide each month in classes IR1, CR1, IR2, CR2, IR5, F1, F2A, FX, F2B, F3 and F4, split by whether the applicant’s nationality appears on the State Department’s 75-country pause list (in effect from 21 January to 21 August 2026).

  • Pause countries: 36% to 48% of all family-based visas in every month from August 2024 to December 2025. In February 2026 they received 372, against 16,663 in February 2025, a 98% fall.
  • Everyone else: 21,849 in February 2026, against 24,600 in February 2025, an 11% fall.
  • Other categories: diversity visa issuances fell to zero in January and February 2026. Employment-based issuances held steady: 7,910 in January and February 2025, 7,934 in the same months of 2026.
  • No later data: as of 20 October 2026 the State Department has not published monthly issuance figures for any month after February 2026, so this table cannot show what happened after the pause ended or after the August interview rescheduling.

How we calculated these figures
  • Denial rate is denials divided by approvals plus denials in the same quarter. USCIS does not publish a rate; we calculate it.
  • USCIS counts every I-130, including petitions for relatives adjusting status inside the US. The tables do not separate consular cases.
  • We leave out USCIS pending counts. USCIS states that administrative closures and transfers are missing from them, so totals cannot be compared across reports.
  • State Department counts go by nationality (foreign state of chargeability or place of birth), not by the embassy that issued the visa. We matched all 75 listed countries to the names in the reports. The rows in each monthly report add up to its published grand total.
  • Immediate-relative visa classes exclude adoption classes (IR3, IR4, IH3, IH4), which come from Forms I-600 and I-800, not the I-130.
