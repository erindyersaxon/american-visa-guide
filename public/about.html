Who I am

My name is Erin. I went through the US Embassy London consular process myself. I know what it's like to have questions at 2am because nothing you've found is specific enough, or because you're not sure you're reading a rule correctly.

I built this site because the information gap is real. Official guides are dense. Community forums are scattered. Good data exists, but it's hard to find and harder to trust.

I am not a lawyer. What I offer is a clear account of how the London process works, drawn from official guidance and from what real applicants have reported. If your case is complex, see when to get a lawyer.

What this site is

Two things: a community data tracker and a process guide.

The tracker shows real timelines from real applicants: more than 250 people have shared their milestones so far. You can see how long people wait at each stage, what outcomes look like, and what has shifted over time. It's historical, not a prediction.

The guide is plain-language documentation of the process: how the I-130 works, what NVC does, and what to expect at the medical and the interview.

What this site is not

It isn't official. American Visa Guide is independent. It is not affiliated with USCIS, the State Department, the US Embassy London or Track My Visa. Everything here is my research and interpretation of public guidance and community experience.

I previously volunteered in the Track My Visa Discord community and have great respect for it. Track My Visa covers every consulate. This site focuses only on the UK-to-US journey through London: London data, London guidance, London resources.

How the data works

People submit milestone dates through a form on the site: when they filed, when they were approved, when they had their interview. Each timeline is stored under a pseudonym you choose, so you can follow your own progress and see where you sit in the community.

Averages leave out extreme outliers. If someone waited 800 days for an I-130 approval because of a security check, their case is still counted, but it doesn't skew the figure everyone else experienced. The aim is to show what the community actually experienced, not what official estimates say to expect.

The data is self-reported and only includes people who chose to share, so treat it as a guide to the typical case, not a promise about yours.

How I keep this accurate

Guidance is based on published government sources, court filings and what applicants report. Official pages are linked so you can check the source yourself.

News is collected daily and every item is read and approved by hand before it appears on the site.

If you spot something wrong or out of date, email me and I'll correct it.

Need a lawyer?

Most straightforward family visa cases do not require an immigration attorney. If your situation is complex: prior visa issues, criminal history, prior removal, previous overstays, or complicated financial sponsorship: professional advice is worthwhile.

American Immigration Lawyers Association (AILA): the professional body for US immigration attorneys. Use their directory to find a qualified lawyer. Be cautious of notarios or immigration consultants who are not licensed attorneys.

Other independent help

Track My Visa is an independent USCIS case tracker. It shows case movement before USCIS's own site does, which helps during the I-130 stage. Their I-130 Consular Processing Discord has channels for the whole consular process at every embassy, even though their tracker covers only the I-130.

Kseniya International is a YouTube channel that clearly explains the I-130, NVC and interview stages for DIY family green card cases. Start with the online I-130 filing guide for spouses, a step-by-step walkthrough of the marriage-based petition.

Both are independent and not affiliated with USCIS or this site.
