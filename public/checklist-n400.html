N-400 preparation list

Tool · Become a US citizen Reviewed 27 Sept 2026

You are here: You are getting ready to apply for US citizenship, or your N-400 is already in progress.

What happens: Work down the list in order. Your ticks are saved in this browser only, so they stay when you come back on the same device.

Do this first: Check your earliest filing date with the calculator.

Checklist

Preparation list

In order, from deciding to file through to the oath ceremony. The N-400 asks about your whole history for the statutory period (3 or 5 years), and some questions cover your whole life. Most delays and denials come from answers that do not match your records. USCIS can deny an application with missing evidence without asking for it first (Policy Alert PA-2026-05). Tick items off as you go: your progress is saved in this browser only.

Talk to an accredited representative or attorney before you file if any of these apply
  • Any arrest, charge or conviction, anywhere in the world, even if it was expunged or sealed. Minor traffic fines under $500 need no court records, unless alcohol or drugs were involved
  • A trip outside the US of more than 6 months during the statutory period
  • You ever claimed to be a US citizen (including on a job form such as the I-9) or registered or voted in a US election
  • Unfiled tax returns, taxes owed, an IRS or state payment plan, an estimated-tax penalty, or late payment during the statutory period, for you or a spouse you file jointly with. Also filing as a "nonresident" since becoming a permanent resident. See Taxes
  • You are a man who did not register with Selective Service between ages 18 and 26 while living in the US
  • You have ever had a fraud or misrepresentation finding, or doubts about how you got your green card

Officers judge good moral character as a whole. They weigh good things, such as work, education, family duties, community work and paying taxes, against any misconduct. They may contact neighbors and employers (PM-602-0188). Filing the N-400 opens your whole immigration record to review. In rare cases a problem can lead to removal proceedings rather than just a denial. [USCIS: Good Moral Character, Vol. 12 Part F]
