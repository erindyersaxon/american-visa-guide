For your visa interview at US Embassy London

Public Charge — Statement Template

A written statement you adapt and send to the embassy before your interview, and bring printed to your interview appointment. It demonstrates your planning for housing, employment, and health insurance — showing the officer that you and your sponsor have thought through financial self-sufficiency. Treat it as a required document.

📍 Who this page is for

This page is written for consular immigrant visa applicants — you filed a DS-260 and you are interviewing at a US embassy or consulate abroad. Everything below describes the consular assessment.

If you are adjusting status inside the United States on Form I-485, this page is not written for you. Adjustment is decided by USCIS under its own published guidance (Policy Manual Volume 8, Part G), which takes effect on 18 September 2026 for applications postmarked or filed electronically on or after that date, and which states in terms that it does not apply to people applying for immigrant visas through the Department of State. Start at USCIS — Public Charge.

⚠️ Treat this as mandatory

The embassy's messaging may describe this statement as something you may voluntarily submit. Do not treat it as optional. Community experience is consistent: cases go more smoothly when this statement has been submitted, and its absence invites exactly the line of questioning it is designed to answer. Prepare it, submit it before your interview, and bring a printed copy — regardless of how the embassy frames it.

📌 What the officer weighs — the beneficiary, not just the sponsor

The assessment used to rest almost entirely on the petitioner's income and the I-864. That is no longer the whole picture. The statute behind it — INA 212(a)(4)(B) — requires the officer to consider five minimum factors, and every one of them is about the beneficiary:

  • Age — considered for its effect on the other factors. If you are at or near retirement age, or state no employment plans, your pension, savings and household resources carry correspondingly more weight.
  • Health — taken from the panel physician's report at your London medical. A Class B condition (one that does not make you inadmissible, but may affect your ability to work or may require extensive care) is the part that feeds this factor.
  • Family status — your household size, and who depends on whom.
  • Assets, resources and financial status — household income first and foremost. Savings and property are counted, and debts are set against them — but see the warning below: at London, assets do not stand in for predictable income.
  • Education and skills — degrees, certifications, licences, and skills gained through work experience, including unpaid and volunteer work. English proficiency sits inside this factor, as do trade qualifications and on-the-job training; formal education is not the only acceptable evidence.

The I-864 is weighed alongside these, not instead of them — a sufficient I-864 no longer ends the enquiry. It cuts the other way too, and sharply: where an I-864 is required, failing to produce a sufficient one is the single thing that sinks a case on its own. Every other factor is weighed in the round, and no one of them decides the outcome by itself.

Community reporting from London is that this beneficiary-focused questioning is now routine at the window. The template below covers all five factors.

⚠️ Assets do not stand in for income at London

Savings, property equity and investments are worth documenting, and they strengthen a case that already has predictable income behind it. They do not replace it. London has repeatedly refused cases resting primarily on assets — including cases that met the I-864 asset multiplier on paper — and required a joint sponsor anyway. This is community-observed across many cases, not an official embassy statement, but it is consistent enough to plan around.

Write your statement so that recurring, predictable income is the answer to "how will this be paid for" in every section — housing, insurance, living costs. Assets belong at the end, as a contingency buffer, and the template below is ordered that way deliberately. Do not lead with them.

If the sponsor's income is short of 125% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines, or sitting near it, secure a joint sponsor rather than leaning on assets — see the I-864 household decision tree.

ℹ️ What this is and why it matters

The officer wants to see that you have thought through housing, employment, and health insurance — and how each will be paid for. A clear, specific plan reads as low risk. No plan at all reads as a red flag. It takes 20 minutes to write and removes an entire line of questioning at interview.

  • Be specific — a real address, a named employer, a named insurance provider. Specific details are more convincing than general plans.
  • Keep it concise — one to two paragraphs per section.
  • Include your case number in both the subject line and body so the unit can match it to your file.
  • Send it early — at least a few days before your interview. Last-minute submissions may not be reviewed in time.
  • Don't overthink it — if your situation is straightforward, say exactly that.
🗣️ Know the answers to these questions

The statement raises specific questions the officer may put to the beneficiary at the window. Whether or not each detail is written into the statement, the beneficiary should be able to answer all of the following out loud and consistently with what was submitted:

  • What does the petitioner do for work? Job title, employer, and roughly what they earn.
  • What does the joint sponsor do for work? Where a joint sponsor is required — their job title, employer, and roughly what they earn.
  • What will the beneficiary do for work in the US? The intended role or sector, and whether a job is already lined up or will be sought after arrival.
  • What is the plan for the beneficiary's health insurance? How the beneficiary will be covered before and after arrival — added to a spouse/family employer plan, a private/short-term policy, or the ACA marketplace.
  • What is the plan for the beneficiary's housing? Where the beneficiary will live on arrival — a confirmed address, or the area and timeline if not yet secured.
  • How will the health insurance and housing be paid for? Which income covers the premiums and the rent/mortgage during any period before the beneficiary is earning.
  • What are the estimated salaries? Roughly what the beneficiary expects to earn in the US, compared with what the petitioner and/or joint sponsor earns — so the officer can see the household is supported throughout.
  • What is the beneficiary's education and work history? Highest qualification, and how long they have worked in their field — continuity matters as much as the current role.
  • Does the beneficiary have savings? Roughly how much is available to draw on during the transition, separate from the sponsor's income.
  • Does the beneficiary have any ongoing health condition? If so, how it is managed and — crucially — who pays for that care in the US.
  • Who else depends on the household? Any children or other dependants travelling with, or already in, the household.
💡 How to submit

Email to LNDIVSubmissions@state.gov with your case number in the subject line, or upload directly to your case in CEAC. Do this at least a few days before your interview date.

My case number is [LND0123456789] and my interview is scheduled for [DATE]. PERSONAL CIRCUMSTANCES I am [age] years old. English is my first language. [If English is not your first language: I am fluent in English — my [education / professional qualifications / current employment] has been conducted in English.] My highest qualification is [qualification] from [institution], awarded in [year]. [Add any that apply: I also hold [professional licence / trade certification / apprenticeship], and I have [X years] of on-the-job training in [skill]. These skills are directly transferable to the US labour market.] I am [married to my petitioner / the [relationship] of my petitioner], and [I have no dependants / my dependants are [names and ages], who are [travelling with me / already in the US]]. I am in good health and have no condition that would prevent me from working. [If you have an ongoing condition: I have [condition], which is well managed with [treatment]. It does not affect my ability to work, and the cost of this care in the US will be covered by [insurance plan / our income] — see the health insurance section below.] HOUSING Upon entry to the United States, I will be living with my [spouse / family member], [Name], at [Full address, City, State, ZIP]. [If address not yet confirmed: We are currently in the process of securing accommodation in [City/State] and expect to confirm our address within [timeframe].] The housing costs will be met by [my petitioner's income / our joint income]. EMPLOYMENT I am a [job title / profession] with [X years] of experience in [sector/industry]. I have been continuously employed in this field since [year], most recently with [Employer name] since [year]. [Choose one:] [Option A — continuing with current employer: I am currently employed by [Employer name] and plan to continue in my role with their US operations following my move. My employer has confirmed this arrangement.] [Option B — seeking employment: I plan to seek employment in [sector] upon arrival. I have [relevant qualifications/experience] and expect to secure employment within [timeframe], at an estimated salary of approximately [$XX,XXX] per year. I have already begun [researching / applying for] roles in [City/State].] My petitioner, [Name], works as a [petitioner job title] at [Employer name], earning approximately [$XX,XXX] per year. [If a joint sponsor is required: My joint sponsor, [Name], works as a [joint sponsor job title] at [Employer name], earning approximately [$XX,XXX] per year.] During any period before my own employment begins, my household will be supported by [my petitioner's / my joint sponsor's] income. The I-864 Affidavit(s) of Support confirm this commitment. HEALTH INSURANCE [Choose one:] [Option A — added to existing plan: I will be added to my [spouse's / family member's] employer-provided health insurance plan through [Employer name] once my Social Security Number is issued. The plan is provided by [Insurance provider] and covers [self and dependents / family].] [Option B — purchasing own plan: I will obtain health insurance through [the Affordable Care Act marketplace / private provider]. I have researched available plans in [State] and expect the monthly premium to be approximately [$XXX].] The insurance premiums will be paid for by [my petitioner's income / our joint income / my own income once employed]. SAVINGS AND ASSETS In addition to the income described above — not in place of it — [I have / my petitioner and I jointly have] savings of approximately [$XX,XXX]. I regard these as a contingency buffer rather than a source of support: they would cover our housing, insurance and living costs for approximately [X months] if they were ever needed. The household costs set out above are met from the income described in each section. [If you have no significant debts: I have no significant outstanding debts or financial liabilities.] [If you do: My outstanding liabilities are [describe], which are serviced from [income source] at [$XXX] per month.] I am committed to supporting myself and my family independently and have no intention of relying on public benefits. I am happy to provide any additional information if required. Yours sincerely, [Beneficiary Full Name] Case Number: [LND0123456789] [Email address]
⚠️ Not legal advice

This template is based on community experience and is not legal advice. Your situation may differ. If you have complex circumstances — prior public benefits use, gaps in employment, medical conditions — consider consulting an immigration attorney before your interview.
