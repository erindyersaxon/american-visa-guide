Section 221(g): how to prevent it, and how to resolve it

A 221(g) is a temporary refusal at the window: the officer needs something before deciding your case. At London it is most often a missing financial document, which you can supply before the interview. This page explains the green refusal sheet box by box, sets out what to do at each stage to keep your case clear of one, and tells you how to respond if you already hold one.

Already holding a green sheet, or called back? Handed a sheet at the window: tick your boxes and get your to-do list, then read if you already have a 221(g). Refused before and now given a new interview date: re-interview after a 221(g).
The short version Upload the IRS tax transcript for every sponsor before the interview. Line up a joint sponsor if income sits anywhere near the line. Make sure the petitioner can show a US domicile. Put every document the officer could ask for into CEAC beforehand. Those four steps prevent the common London financial 221(g).

What a 221(g) is

Section 221(g) of the Immigration and Nationality Act lets a consular officer refuse a visa while the application is incomplete or needs further review, without denying it on the merits. It is a temporary refusal. In most London cases it means the officer needs something that is not yet on file. Supply it, and the case resumes.

At London the officer hands you a green Consular Section: Immigrant Visa Unit sheet, with the relevant boxes ticked and the submission address LNDIVSubmissions@state.gov printed at the top. The ticked boxes are the whole of what you must clear. The sheet divides 221(g) into two types, and the type decides what happens next:

Type 1 Additional administrative processing

A mandatory background or eligibility review. The sheet says no one can expedite or waive it, it has no set timeframe, and you cannot check its status: the Embassy contacts you when it finishes. You often have nothing to submit, though some posts ask for your personal history and CV: see administrative processing. It is the less common type, and you control less of it.

Type 2 Submission of additional evidence

The officer needs the ticked documents before processing can continue. This is the common London 221(g), it most often concerns money, and this page aims to help you avoid it. You control the outcome: send what is ticked, and the case moves again.

A 221(g) is not a denial The officer has not refused your case on the merits. Most cases resolve once you supply the requested item. Each one still adds weeks to months of delay, which is why prevention repays the effort.

Every box that can appear on the sheet

The two pages carry the checklist below. Only the ticked items apply to you. Knowing what the form can ask for tells you what to have ready before interview day.

Ticked boxWhat it requires
Petition returned to USCIS The post has sent your petition back to USCIS for review. Send enquiries to the USCIS office that decided it, not the Embassy.
Ineligible under INA 212(a) The officer found a ground of inadmissibility. A waiver (Form I-212 or I-601) may be possible, and USCIS decides it. ESTA treats this as a visa denial.
Refused under 221(g) The core refusal box, with two sub-types: additional administrative processing (mandatory, no timeframe) or submission of additional evidence (the ticked documents below).
Affidavit of Support (I-864 / I-864EZ) The petitioner's or sponsor's I-864 is missing or deficient. If you count a household member's income, add Form I-864A. Any new I-864 signed from 1 October 2026 must be the 08/24/26 edition. A sponsor's credit freeze can hold up the sufficiency check, and that delay can land here.
IRS transcript or tax return (1040) An IRS transcript, or a photocopy of the signed return, for the sponsor's most recent tax year. If the sponsor filed no return, a signed statement explaining why.
Evidence of personal assets Bank statements, real estate, stocks, bonds, insurance policies and investments, for cases where income alone falls short. See the Form I-864 instructions.
Sponsor below the minimum income (213A) The classic London financial box. You need a joint sponsor, who provides an I-864 (one per applicant), an IRS transcript or 1040 with their latest W-2, proof of US citizenship or LPR status, and an I-864A if needed. The joint sponsor must be 18 or over, a US citizen or LPR, and domiciled in the US.
Proof of US domicile Evidence that the sponsor lives in the US: US tax filings, US property, a signed US lease, US bank accounts, utility bills or a state ID. See the domicile problem.
Recent employer job letter On letterhead, dated within 12 months, giving job title, salary and dates of employment. A job transfer letter or signed job offer also works.
Intent-to-marry letter (K-1) A letter from the petitioner confirming that you will marry within 90 days of your arrival in the US.
School leaving certificate From the named school.
Police certificate From the named country or authority. For the UK, an ACRO certificate.
Long-form birth certificate For the named person, with a certified translation if needed.
Original marriage / divorce certificate For the named person, with a certified translation if needed.
Medical examination results Book with Visa Medicals (020 7486 7822). The clinic sends results to the Embassy. Results last 6 months, and you must enter the US within 6 months of the exam.
Passport validity A passport valid for at least six months beyond your expected entry date. That is London's rule. The regulation, 22 CFR 42.64(b), asks for 60 days beyond the visa's expiry, but an immigrant visa can run for six months, so meet London's rule.
Other A free-text line for anything else.

The public charge box

The London checklist above predates the change in how officers assess public charge. Posts have since added a public charge heading to the 221(g) form, with its own document list. Below is the version Consulate General Lagos issued in 2026. It is not a London document, and we have no confirmation that London uses the same wording. We show it because a public charge refusal asks for the same documents wherever it happens, and because the list doubles as a preparation checklist. See what is confirmed, and what is not.

Community-supplied copy of a checklist issued at Consulate General Lagos, 2026. It carries no case number or applicant name.

The preamble

Your case requires additional documentation to confirm that you overcome public charge concerns. You may provide any evidence you possess to show that your petitioner or a joint sponsor will be willing and able to provide for your financial needs, including maintenance, housing and healthcare. While you are free to submit any evidence you want, we specifically request the following documents.

The list asks for: tax transcripts for the most recent year, the two latest pay stubs, the latest W-2, evidence of the petitioner's other income, health insurance, savings, investments and retirement accounts, the beneficiary's own savings listed apart from the petitioner's, the petitioner's lease or property tax records, your work history and education history as lists, your work plans in the US, a health insurance price quote for your age in the state where you will live, more detail on how the joint sponsor relates to the petitioner, and a new joint sponsor I-864 with proof of status.

Have all of it before the interview. A case that arrives with the whole list leaves the officer nothing to request. The evidence table on the public charge page says which items to send with your statement and which to bring.

If you get one: respond once, by the route you are given

Send your response the way the post's follow-up email tells you to, and by no other route. Posts differ: the Lagos checklist tells applicants to upload to CEAC and then email the post, while London's follow-up emails have given an upload link, a mailing address or both. Follow your own email. See if you already have a 221(g).

Posts email you once they finish reviewing your submission, and state that given the number of cases in administrative processing they cannot expedite your case. Send everything the first time and then wait. A partial response restarts the clock, and chasing does not move it.

The most common cause: money

Community reports show most London 221(g)s concern money. Two patterns account for most of them, and you can avoid both before interview day.

1. The missing IRS tax transcript NVC accepts either an IRS tax transcript or a complete copy of the filed return. London does not always agree at the interview. Officers have issued 221(g)s for a missing transcript after NVC granted DQ, with the full return, W-2 and pay stubs on file. This applies to each financial sponsor on the case, joint sponsors included. DQ on a return copy is not the finish line. The interview is.
2. Income judged too low, with no joint sponsor ready London officers doubt cases that rest on income near the 125% poverty-guideline threshold, or on assets alone. Officers have judged income “too low” at the window and asked for a joint sponsor where the figures cleared the guideline on paper. Cases that rely on savings, property equity or investments have drawn a 221(g) despite meeting the asset test. If your margin is thin, or you lean on assets, have a joint sponsor ready.

The rule underneath both: where NVC's minimum and London's practice differ, meet London's standard before you submit. Reaching DQ on lesser evidence and then drawing a 221(g) for the same document costs far more time.

The petitioner's domicile problem

A joint sponsor can fix income. Nobody can fix the petitioner's domicile. It is the one financial problem on this page that a joint sponsor does not solve, and the guidance officers follow says so.

A joint sponsor cannot cure it “If a petitioner cannot satisfy the domicile requirement, the petitioner fails to qualify as a ‘sponsor’ for the purposes of submitting Form I-864. A joint sponsor cannot be accepted and the applicant must be refused pursuant to INA 212(a)(4).” (9 FAM 601.14-7(a)(3)(c))

A case can survive low petitioner income: a joint sponsor files alongside, and the case goes on. A petitioner without a US domicile is a different problem, because the petitioner is the sponsor and no second filer can supply it. It catches couples living in the UK more than most, because living together in the UK while the case runs is the natural choice.

Domicile means your principal residence is in the United States and you intend to keep it there. You need both halves. Find your position below.

Your situationWhere you stand
You live in the US Nothing to solve. Carry the ordinary evidence: US tax filings, utility bills, state ID.
You are abroad for a set period and kept your US home Show that you left for a limited, and not indefinite, period, that you meant to keep your US domicile when you went, and that real ties continue. The guidance names students, contract workers and NGO volunteers as model cases. “Limited, not indefinite” is what catches couples: moving abroad to be with your partner, with no end date, does not count as a temporary absence, whatever US accounts you hold.
You are abroad on qualifying US employment The guidance already treats you as US-domiciled. See the box below: some petitioners qualify without knowing it.
You are abroad and your US domicile has lapsed You must re-establish it. The officer must be satisfied that you have taken real steps and have taken up US residence, or will by the date the person you sponsor immigrates. You may travel together. You may not follow later.
Check this before you assume you have a problem A US citizen living abroad for a set period is domiciled in the US if employed by: the US government; a recognised US research institution; a US firm or corporation engaged in whole or in part in developing US foreign trade or commerce, or a subsidiary of one; a public international organisation the US joins by treaty or statute; or a US religious denomination, as authorised clergy or a missionary (9 FAM 601.14-7(b), INA 319(b)(1)). The third item reaches further than petitioners expect. An LPR sponsor abroad also counts as US-domiciled if they obtained the preservation of residence benefit under INA 316(b) or 317.

What counts as evidence

The Department lists what establishes domicile: opening a US bank account, moving funds to the US, making US investments, seeking US employment, registering children in US schools, applying for a Social Security number, and voting in local, State or Federal elections. Property, a mortgage and a lease are missing from that list.

A lease proves less than you think The green sheet lists property and leases, so include yours. But a lease or mortgage shows an address, not a residence: a landlord does not know whether you are in the country. Do not let it stand alone. The evidence that carries weight comes from living there: US tax filings showing domestic earned income, a W-2, a US job contract or signed offer with a start date, utility bills in your name across several months, and school or university enrollment. The officer asks whether you have “in fact taken up principal residence” in the United States.

If you are starting from nothing, two points help. Your US address can be a house, an apartment, or an arrangement to stay with family or friends, so you need no lease for that part. And no fixed deadline applies: the officer must be satisfied that you have taken up residence.

Citizenship is not domicile A US citizen who has lived abroad since childhood may have no adult US domicile to point to: no US earned income, no state ID, no utility record, perhaps no Social Security number and no voting record. That rules out four of the seven items on the Department's list from a standing start. If that is you, begin at the I-130 stage, not at the interview.
If you are the petitioner on a K-1 K visa applicants file an I-134 at the interview, not an I-864, so this section does not describe your paperwork. It does describe your risk. A petitioner outside the United States weakens the public charge picture and the claim that you will marry and live there, and the full I-864 domicile requirement arrives when your fiancé(e) adjusts status. You defer it; you do not avoid it.

The NVC checklist, the binder checklist and the interview checklist all carry the domicile items.

Prevent it: what to do at each stage

Most 221(g)s trace back to a gap you could have seen earlier. Close these at each stage, and the officer has nothing left to request at the window.

Stage 1 · I-130 Plan; nothing to submit yet
  • If the US citizen petitioner lives overseas, plan how they will show a US domicile, up to moving back, job hunting and relocation. It needs to be in place by the time the petition reaches NVC. Read the domicile problem now: a joint sponsor cannot cover it, and a petitioner abroad for years has the most ground to make up.
  • Ask whether you may need a joint sponsor, for example if the petitioner's income is low, they are a student, they earn no W-2 or self-employment income, or they live overseas with you.
Stage 2 · NVC Meet London's bar, not the DQ minimum
  • Upload the IRS tax transcript for each sponsor and joint sponsor, from irs.gov/individuals/get-transcript, even if NVC granted DQ on a 1040 copy. No other step prevents more 221(g)s.
  • If the transcript is not ready, upload the complete signed 1040, W-2s, pay stubs and the IRS e-file acceptance, and note in CEAC that the transcript will follow before the interview.
  • If income is marginal, provide a full joint sponsor package: their own I-864, transcript, proof of US status and proof of US domicile.
  • Ask each sponsor and I-864A household member to check for a credit freeze before signing, and to lift it when asked. The 08/24/26 edition authorizes a credit check on each of them.
  • Upload domicile evidence for the petitioner even where NVC does not ask for it, and do not rest it on a lease: US tax filings showing domestic earned income, a W-2 or US job offer with a start date, utility bills, a state ID. If the petitioner lives abroad, add evidence that they are re-establishing domicile.

More in the NVC stage of the guide and the NVC checklist.

Stage 3 · Waiting for your interview letter Refresh everything before interview day
  • If a tax-filing deadline has passed since DQ, upload the newest year's transcript to CEAC so the officer sees current figures.
  • Check that your passport is valid for at least six months beyond your intended entry date, and renew it now if not.
  • Get your ACRO police certificate (the Embassy accepts it for 12 months) and make sure you can reach your civil document originals.
  • Send a public charge statement with its evidence. The embassy calls it voluntary; treat it as mandatory. The statement builder writes it for you.
Stage 4 · Medical & document check Do not count on document check to catch your gaps

Embassy staff check your documents on the day of your medical, about two weeks before the interview. They sometimes flag a missing document, but they miss things.

  • Community members have passed document check with real gaps unflagged, such as a missing IRS transcript, missing domicile evidence or an unmet need for a joint sponsor, and then drawn a 221(g) for the same item at the interview. Had staff flagged them at document check, the couple could have fixed each gap in the two weeks before the interview.
  • Treat document check as a backstop. Run your own check against the boxes on the green sheet, and arrive interview-ready whether or not staff flag anything.
Stage 5 · Interview day Everything already in CEAC
  • Get each financial document into CEAC before interview day. No rule bars evidence at the window, but officers decide from what is in CEAC, and a document you produce for the first time in the room can draw a 221(g) asking you to submit it through the proper route. Bring originals, but do not rely on handing them over.
  • One exception: a paper copy of something you already uploaded to CEAC can rescue a case when staff cannot see the upload. This has saved cases at document check.
  • Make your answers match your DS-260 and CEAC. Officers probe inconsistencies.

If you already have a 221(g)

Work the ticked boxes, and only the ticked boxes.

  1. Read what is ticked. That is the whole of what you must clear. Extra material slows review. The green sheet triage turns your ticked boxes into a to-do list.
  2. Wait for the follow-up email. Within one or two days of the interview, the post emails submission instructions to the address you registered with the courier service.
  3. Submit by the route the email gives, whether an upload link, a mailing address or something else, and by no other.
  4. Send everything at once. If the officer asked for several documents, wait until you have them all. Use the email template.
  5. Act within a year. The sheet warns that if you do not act within one year of the date on the letter, the post cancels the application under INA §203(g). Submit as soon as your documents are ready.
Do not fall back on CEAC, and post only what the email asks for

Unless the email tells you to use CEAC, do not upload your response there. Community members who uploaded 221(g) evidence to CEAC after a London interview, without instruction, report the documents sitting at Submitted for weeks with the case stalled.

Do not generate a courier QR code in AIS unless the email asks for physical documents. One applicant believes that doing so unprompted led the post to require everything by post.

What happens next

Your passport. If you tell the officer you can return the documents within days, they may keep your passport, which shortens the turnaround. Promise a fast return only if you can deliver it. If the officer returned your passport, expect a request to send it once your documents are in: in one 2025 London case it came the same day as the upload.

A mailing confirmation. Where the email asks for documents by post, GDIT, which runs the visa appointment service, emails a confirmation once the courier's service center receives them. One applicant's read, in part: “The documents you delivered to the service center at Mail Boxes Etc. Holborn have been received and will be forwarded to the U.S. Consular Section for processing.”

One London 221(g), start to finish (2025)
  • 14 November: interview, 221(g)
  • 17 November: follow-up email with submission instructions
  • 18 November: documents uploaded through the link in the email; a request for the passport arrives the same day
  • 20 November: case updated; the embassy receives the passport
  • 12 December: visa issued
  • 15 December: courier tracking email
  • 17 December: passport delivered
  • 18 December: flight to the US

About three and a half weeks from documents to visa, and five from interview to arrival. One case, not a promise.

If administrative processing is ticked instead of a document list, you often have nothing to submit. No one can expedite or waive it, and it has no published timeframe; the Embassy contacts you when it finishes. Add LNDIVSubmissions@state.gov and the courier's senders to your safe-senders list. If the post asks for your personal history and CV, see administrative processing.

If the reason you were given is vague. Cable 26 STATE 88862 (10 September 2026) tells officers who enter a new 221(g) to note the specific reasons and to spell out any reason, in the case notes and to the applicant. The instruction targets cases reworked after the vacated immigrant visa and diversity visa pauses, so it does not bind every 221(g). It still shows the standard the Department expects of its officers: if your stated reason is vague, say so when you follow up with the post. The cable is public as ECF 95-1 in Medani v. Trump, No. 5:26-cv-06332 (N.D. Cal.).

Called back to interview

If you received a 221(g) before the September 2026 interview cancellations and now have a new interview date, read re-interview after a 221(g). In short:

  • An ordinary 221(g) does not lock in the old standard. The officer decides public charge at the new interview, with the new worksheet, and can look at all five factors about you, beyond the box ticked on your old sheet.
  • A pause-only 221(g) went back a step, not to the start, but the officer may still ask public charge questions.
  • The officer assesses anything that has changed, for both kinds. Above all, check whether the petitioner has moved out of the US, which a joint sponsor cannot fix. Check your medical too: results last 6 months, and you must enter the US within that time.

Administrative processing and technology checks

Some 221(g)s tick administrative processing and then ask for 15 years of travel, addresses and employment, plus a CV. One report from another post, in September 2026, followed an interview with detailed questions about the beneficiary's cybersecurity degree. The form matches the Department's DS-5535 security questionnaire, and the request points to a security or technology-transfer review, not a public charge finding.

If you studied or work in a dual-use field, such as chemistry, microbiology, cybersecurity, aerospace, drones, remote sensing, AI or advanced materials, the officer may refer your case to Washington for a technology check before deciding it. Officers listen for key words from the Technology Alert List. Prepare a precise CV, a publications list and an employer letter, and describe your work in plain, accurate terms.

221(g) vs administrative processing vs the visa pause

Applicants mix these up. They differ:

WhatWhat it means
221(g) refusal A formal refusal at the window, on the green sheet, pending documents or review. It resolves when you supply the ticked items or the review finishes.
Routine post-approval AP A short “Administrative Processing” status in CEAC during final checks and visa printing after a successful interview. This is normal and is not a 221(g).
221(g) with a history request Administrative processing ticked at the window, then a request for 15 years of personal history and a CV. A security or technical review, not a public charge refusal. See administrative processing.
Visa pause A federal court struck down the January 2026 nationality visa pause on 21 August 2026, and set aside refusals based only on it for a fresh decision. See the nationality visa pause, struck down. The ruling does not touch the separate travel ban, Proclamation 10998, which still suspends immigrant visas for nationals of 39 countries with no general exception for relatives of US citizens. If you also hold a passport from a country on neither list, check whether the proclamation's dual-national exception applies, and upload that passport to CEAC.
