Policy status · September 2026

Where things stand

Four recent changes affect anyone filing Form N-400 now. Check each against the linked source before you file, because naturalization policy has changed several times in the past year.

Incomplete filings can now be denied without warning

Since 5 August 2026 (Policy Alert PA-2026-05), USCIS officers may deny a request that lacks required initial evidence without first sending a Request for Evidence (RFE) or Notice of Intent to Deny (NOID). This applies to pending and new filings. File a complete package the first time. [USCIS: Policy Manual updates]

Proposed fee increase: not yet in force

On 23 June 2026 DHS proposed raising the N-400 fee to $1,330 on paper and $1,280 online, and ending both the $380 reduced fee and fee waivers (military applicants stay exempt). Comments closed on 24 August 2026. The rule is not final, so today's fees still apply. The fee is set by the date USCIS receives your application. [Federal Register 2026-12542]

The 2025 civics test

For an N-400 filed on or after 20 October 2025, the officer asks up to 20 of 128 questions and you must answer 12 correctly. If USCIS received your N-400 before that date, you take the older 2008 test instead. [USCIS: 2025 Civics Test]

Good moral character and "high-risk country" holds

Since August 2025, officers have weighed positive contributions and community ties as well as the absence of misconduct (PM-602-0188). Separately, the holds on applications from nationals of 39 designated countries (PM-602-0192 and PM-602-0194) were blocked by a federal court on 24 August 2026 (Red Eagle Law v. Edlow, N.D. Cal.). [USCIS: Court order on hold policies]

Checklist

Preparation list

In order, from deciding to file through to the oath ceremony. The N-400 asks about your whole history for the statutory period (3 or 5 years), and some questions cover your whole life. Most delays and denials come from answers that do not match your records. Tick items off as you go: your progress is saved in this browser only.

Talk to an accredited representative or attorney before you file if any of these apply
  • Any arrest, charge or conviction, anywhere in the world, even if it was expunged or sealed. Minor traffic fines under $500 need no court records, unless alcohol or drugs were involved
  • A trip outside the US of more than 6 months during the statutory period
  • You ever claimed to be a US citizen (including on a job form such as the I-9) or registered or voted in a US election
  • Unfiled tax returns, taxes owed, or filing as a "nonresident" since becoming a permanent resident
  • You are a man who did not register with Selective Service between ages 18 and 26 while living in the US
  • You have ever had a fraud or misrepresentation finding, or doubts about how you got your green card

Filing the N-400 opens your whole immigration record to review. In rare cases a problem can lead to removal proceedings rather than just a denial. [USCIS: Good Moral Character, Vol. 12 Part F]

Study tool · civics and English tests

Civics test flashcards

All 128 questions and answers of the 2025 civics test, word for word from the USCIS list "128 Civics Questions and Answers (2025 version)" (M-1778, 09/25). It applies to every N-400 filed on or after 20 October 2025. You need 12 correct out of up to 20. The test is spoken: the officer reads each question aloud and you answer aloud. The officer stops once you have enough correct answers to pass, or once you can no longer pass. For each question, give one listed answer, or as many as the question asks for.

Filed before 20 October 2025?

If USCIS received your N-400 before 20 October 2025, you take the older 2008 civics test: up to 10 of 100 questions, 6 correct to pass. These flashcards do not cover it. Study from the 2008 list of 100 questions and the other 2008 materials on the USCIS study page.

Answers that change, and answers that depend on where you live

Some answers depend on who holds office when you interview: the President, Vice President, Speaker of the House, and Chief Justice. Answer with whoever holds the office on your interview day, and check uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates the week before. Four answers depend on where you live: your senators, your representative, your governor and your state capital. Look them up for your address. The cards give current names as a study note, separate from the official answer.

    The English test

    The English test has three parts. Speaking is judged from how you answer questions about your N-400 during the interview. For reading, you must read aloud one of up to three sentences correctly. For writing, you must write one of up to three sentences correctly, as the officer reads it to you. Every word in the reading and writing sentences comes from the two USCIS lists below. If you fail the English or civics test, you get a second attempt at the part you failed, usually 60 to 90 days later. [USCIS Policy Manual Vol. 12 Part E]

    Source: USCIS, Reading Vocabulary and Writing Vocabulary for the Naturalization Test (rev. 07/14). USCIS also publishes printable vocabulary flash cards (M-715), word strips and copy sheets (M-1178) for practising at home or in class.

    Help with the fee

    • Reduced fee ($380): if your household income is between 150% and 400% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines. Request it on the paper N-400 with proof of income. It cannot be requested online
    • Fee waiver ($0): file Form I-912 with a paper N-400 if you receive a means-tested benefit, your household income is at or below 150% of the guidelines, or you face financial hardship. Do not send the fee as well: USCIS will take the payment instead of considering the waiver
    • Military: no fee for current and former service members applying under INA 328 or 329
    • Both the reduced fee and fee waivers would end under the proposed fee rule. If you qualify, filing before a final rule takes effect preserves them

    Source: USCIS Fee Schedule (G-1055); USCIS: Form N-400.
